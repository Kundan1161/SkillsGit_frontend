# Inpainting and Outpainting Workflow

## When to use

Use this skill when a contributor needs to *change part of an image while preserving the rest* (inpainting) or *extend an image beyond its current canvas* (outpainting). Inpainting fixes defects (extra fingers, mis-shapen objects, awkward background), removes unwanted elements, swaps an object for another, and refines a region the rest of the image got right. Outpainting extends a photograph or generation to a new aspect ratio, adds plausible environmental context, or stitches multiple frames into a wider composition.

The plan covers mask construction (source, quality, feather, granularity), the inpaint mode (latent inpaint, pixel inpaint, inpaint-conditioning model, full-frame regenerate with mask blending), the denoise strength schedule, the seed strategy for variant exploration vs deterministic refinement, the seam-blending policy that hides the edit, the iteration ladder for converging on a clean result, and the composite-preservation discipline that keeps logos, brand colours, recognisable faces, and downstream-locked elements unchanged.

The skill is downstream of `ai-image-prompt-designer` (which composes the prompt for the changed region) and `controlnet-conditioning-architect` (which can lock structural attributes inside the masked region). It is appropriate for SD 1.5, SDXL, SD 3.x, and Flux-class base models; mode availability differs by family and is called out in the plan.

**Mandatory safety disclaimer.** This skill produces methodology guidance for diffusion-based image generation. The skill does not address the copyright status of training data or generated outputs in any jurisdiction; consult counsel for commercial use. The skill does not produce or recommend NSFW, defamatory, infringing, or person-impersonating content; usage must respect platform policy and applicable law.

## How to apply

The skill walks an eleven-stage pipeline. Early stages establish intent and the preservation contract. Middle stages design the mask, choose the inpaint mode, set denoise, and draft the prompt. Late stages handle seams, iteration, composite preservation, and the deliverable.

### Stage 1 — Establish the preservation contract

1. From `edit_intent` derive two lists: the *change region* (what may move, recolour, regenerate) and the *preservation region* (what must remain pixel-identical). The preservation list often includes faces, logos, brand colours, sharp text, fingerprintable patterns, and any element that downstream consumers will compare against the original.
2. Define preservation tolerance per element. "Logo must remain pixel-identical." "Face position must remain within one pixel; texture may regenerate within 95% similarity." "Background gradient must remain perceptually identical, exact pixels not required." Tolerances drive whether a region needs hard masking, soft masking, or a composite restore.
3. Identify *risk regions* — areas near the change region where bleed is likely. Soft-edge masks bleed; latent inpaint at high denoise bleeds; outpaint seams bleed. Risk regions get explicit seam policy.
4. If the change region overlaps a person's face that must remain recognisable as a specific identity, the plan calls out that diffusion inpaint changes identity unless explicitly conditioned; for identity preservation, recommend identity-locking via IP-Adapter-face, a subject LoRA, or a strict pixel-restore composite outside this workflow.

### Stage 2 — Decide inpaint vs outpaint vs both

5. *Inpaint.* The frame stays the same; a region inside is regenerated. Use when the canvas is correct and only content within the frame is wrong.
6. *Outpaint.* The frame grows; new content is generated outside the original area. Use when the canvas itself must extend.
7. *Both.* Some workflows do an outpaint pass followed by an inpaint pass to fix the seam region or refine a specific element in the extension. Order matters — extend first, refine second.
8. *Iterative refinement without an explicit mask.* "Img2img with low denoise on the full frame" is a third pattern. It refines globally rather than locally. Use for polish (textures, palette unification, mild detail enhancement); avoid for surgical edits.

### Stage 3 — Construct the mask

9. The mask declares which pixels are eligible to change. Mask quality dominates output quality more than any single hyperparameter.
10. *Source.*
    - Hand-painted in an editor — full control, slowest.
    - SAM-derived (Segment Anything) — fast and accurate for objects, people, and well-defined regions; needs cleanup at boundaries.
    - Threshold-derived (luma, chroma key) — fast for solid-background work; brittle.
    - Generated from a depth or segmentation map — useful when the change is "everything beyond depth N" or "everything labelled sky."
11. *Granularity.* Match the mask to the smallest unit that must change. A whole-face mask for a hand fix wastes signal and risks identity drift; a tight hand-only mask preserves identity by construction.
12. *Coverage.* Slightly oversize the mask to include the contact boundary between change and preserve. Undersized masks leave halos of broken structure at the edge.
13. *Feather.* A 3-15 pixel feather (proportional to resolution) softens the transition. Hard masks (no feather) are appropriate for cut-line precision (silhouette work) but produce visible seams when the inpaint changes lighting or colour.
14. *Multi-region masks.* For multiple discrete edits, separate masks per region with separate prompts and denoise strengths are usually better than one big mask with one prompt.
15. *Mask inversion check.* Always confirm which polarity the runner expects. Inverted masks ("white = preserve" vs "white = change") are a perennial silent failure.

### Stage 4 — Choose the inpaint mode

16. *Latent inpaint with an inpaint-trained model.* Highest quality for diffusion-native inpainting. Use an inpaint-trained checkpoint (often a fine-tuned base) or an inpaint adapter where available. Best preservation of unmasked pixels and best seam behaviour.
17. *Latent inpaint with a base model and mask blending.* Works without an inpaint-trained model. Quality is usable but unmasked region can drift slightly because the latent is partially re-noised. Common in workflows that have only a non-inpaint checkpoint.
18. *Pixel inpaint via paste-back.* Generate a full-frame img2img pass, then mask-blend back to the original pixels outside the mask. Simple, robust, preserves unmasked pixels exactly. Best when preservation is paramount and slight imperfection inside the mask is acceptable.
19. *Inpaint-conditioning ControlNet.* Use a ControlNet variant trained on inpaint conditioning. Stronger preservation than pure latent inpaint; especially useful with SDXL and Flux-class.
20. *Differential diffusion.* Continuous mask values map to continuous denoise strengths per pixel; produces very smooth blending. Useful for soft edits (replace texture in a region, gradually).
21. *Outpaint with progressive extension.* Outpaint a small strip at a time rather than doubling the canvas in one pass. Each strip is small enough that the model still has context from the unchanged area to match colour, lighting, and content.

### Stage 5 — Choose denoise strength

22. Denoise (sometimes called "strength") is the fraction of noise the latent inpaint adds to the masked region before re-denoising. Range 0.0 to 1.0.
23. *Low denoise (0.2-0.4).* Subtle change. Useful for polish — clean up texture, smooth a transition, gently nudge a colour. The model preserves most of the original.
24. *Medium denoise (0.4-0.7).* Moderate change. The masked region is meaningfully redrawn but retains some of the original's structure. Sweet spot for many surgical edits when the original region has some usable substrate.
25. *High denoise (0.7-1.0).* Full regeneration. The masked region is drawn afresh. Use when the original is junk (extra fingers, blown-out face, missing object) and there is no substrate to preserve.
26. For outpaint, the new region has no substrate — denoise is effectively 1.0 in the extension and lower in the overlap strip. Most outpainting workflows use a graduated denoise across the overlap.
27. The plan records a *denoise sweep* — three candidates around the chosen default — for the iteration ladder.

### Stage 6 — Compose the prompt for the masked region

28. The prompt for an inpaint pass should describe *the masked region only* and the *connection to the unmasked region*. "A small clean ceramic kettle handle, matching the kettle's surface, soft window light from upper left, no shadow on the surface below" is operational.
29. Include a *context band* — describing the unchanged surrounding context — so the model regenerates the mask consistently with its environment. "Surrounded by a wooden countertop and stone backsplash" anchors the inpaint to the rest of the frame.
30. Negative prompt is the same shape as in `ai-image-prompt-designer` but scoped to the masked region's expected defects. For hand fixes, include extra-finger and fused-finger terms; for face fixes, include doubled-feature terms; for material fixes, include over-shiny / plastic terms.
31. For outpaint, prompt for the *extended environment*. Describe what should plausibly continue beyond the original frame; do not introduce new subjects unless explicitly intended.
32. Resolution discipline: prompt as if the masked region is its own image. The model treats the masked latent as the canvas; descriptors of size and framing should match the masked region's effective size, not the full frame's.

### Stage 7 — Sampler, CFG, steps, seed

33. Inherit the family's defaults from `ai-image-prompt-designer`. Inpaint-trained checkpoints often respond well to slightly lower CFG (one step below the base default) — they are already biased toward the unmasked content.
34. Use deterministic samplers when the iteration ladder will vary one parameter at a time. Ancestral samplers add stochasticity that masks parameter effects.
35. Seeds: exploratory batch of 6-8 seeds at default denoise to find a candidate region match; lock the seed for refinement.
36. For outpaint progressive extension, the seed for the seam strip can be locked across strips to maintain colour consistency; for the new fully-extended region, a fresh seed each strip is fine.

### Stage 8 — Seam blending policy

37. *Soft mask + matched denoise.* The default — feathered mask in the 3-15 pixel range with denoise low enough that lighting matches. Resolves most seams.
38. *Overlap strip with low-denoise refinement.* After the primary inpaint, run a second low-denoise pass over a narrow strip straddling the seam to harmonise texture and grain.
39. *Pixel paste-back with feather.* For preservation-strict workflows, paste the original unmasked pixels back over the result with a feathered alpha. Slowest but tightest preservation.
40. *Match colour and tone explicitly.* When the inpaint changes the local colour palette, apply a colour-match pass (histogram match or LAB-space colour transfer from a swatch of the unmasked region) before the seam refinement.
41. *Lighting-aware seam.* When the seam crosses a strong lighting boundary (window light spilling across the seam), do not treat the seam as a uniform feather; mask the seam strip into "lit" and "unlit" parts and refine separately.
42. *Outpaint seams require horizon and gradient continuity.* Sky gradient, horizon line, ground texture must continue across the seam. The plan recommends including a control (canny or depth) along the seam edge for the outpaint pass to enforce continuity.

### Stage 9 — Iteration ladder

43. *Step 1 — mask audit.* Render the mask as an overlay; confirm coverage, feather, and polarity. Bad mask is the most common defect; fix it before tuning denoise.
44. *Step 2 — denoise sweep at default sampler.* Run the prompt at low / medium / high denoise candidates. Pick the candidate that resolves the defect without disturbing preservation.
45. *Step 3 — prompt refinement.* If the candidate denoise produces structurally close output with wrong details, refine the prompt's masked-region band and re-run at the same denoise.
46. *Step 4 — sampler / CFG swap.* If detail texture is wrong, swap sampler or CFG.
47. *Step 5 — seam pass.* Run the seam-blending policy. Inspect at 1:1 zoom.
48. *Step 6 — composite restore.* Paste preserved-region pixels back where preservation tolerance demanded pixel-identity.
49. *Step 7 — full-frame polish (optional).* A whole-frame low-denoise img2img (denoise 0.15-0.25) can harmonise overall grain and palette. Skip if preservation tolerance forbids it.
50. *Step 8 — escalate.* If the ladder ends without convergence, the issue is structural (mask is wrong, model lacks the concept, control conditioning is required). Escalate to `controlnet-conditioning-architect` or `lora-and-finetune-methodology`.

### Stage 10 — Composite preservation and audit

51. After the workflow completes, compute a *preservation audit*: for each element on the preservation list, measure similarity (pixel diff for hard preservation, perceptual metric for soft preservation) against the original. Out-of-tolerance elements trigger a composite restore.
52. For face preservation, an explicit identity check is recommended — a face-similarity score between original and edited image must remain above a project-specific threshold.
53. For logo preservation, exact pixel match is the rule. Logos near the change region should be masked into the preservation set and pixel-restored after the workflow.
54. Document every audit pass alongside the asset metadata for downstream verification.

### Stage 11 — Compose the deliverable

55. Open with a one-paragraph *edit intent* statement: what changes, what is preserved, on what source, against what tolerance.
56. Render the plan as a markdown document covering the preservation contract, mask construction, inpaint mode, denoise strategy, prompt structure, sampler / CFG / seed, seam policy, iteration ladder, composite preservation, and the audit protocol.
57. Emit `plan_json` with: `intent`, `preservation` (elements, tolerances), `mask` (source, granularity, coverage, feather, polarity), `inpaint_type`, `model_choice`, `prompt`, `negative_prompt`, `denoise` (default, sweep), `sampler`, `cfg`, `steps`, `seed_strategy`, `seam_policy`, `iteration_ladder`, `audit`, `risk_notes`.
58. Close with the mandatory safety disclaimer and a "what this skill does not cover" note pointing the contributor at `ai-image-prompt-designer`, `controlnet-conditioning-architect`, and `production-batch-rendering-pipeline` as appropriate.

## Examples

**Input (placeholder):**

`edit_intent`: "Hero shot of a model holding our product. The right hand is malformed (six fingers). Need to fix the hand without touching the face, the product, or the studio backdrop."

`source_state`: "Generated image at 1024x1536, PNG, no alpha; face is preserved-tolerance pixel-identical, product preserved-tolerance pixel-identical, backdrop soft preservation."

`model_and_runtime`: "SDXL inpaint-trained checkpoint available; one 24 GB GPU."

`failure_modes`: "Earlier inpaint at denoise 0.9 destroyed wrist and changed sleeve cuff; earlier inpaint at denoise 0.5 still showed six fingers."

**Plan (abbreviated):**

- Intent: regenerate the right hand only; preserve face and product pixel-identically, soft-preserve backdrop.
- Mode: latent inpaint with SDXL inpaint-trained checkpoint.
- Mask: SAM-derived hand outline, cleaned up to include only fingers, palm, and 2-pixel ring of cuff; 6-pixel feather; polarity confirmed (white = change); face, product, backdrop masked out.
- Denoise: sweep 0.55 / 0.65 / 0.75; expected sweet spot 0.65.
- Prompt (masked region band): "a relaxed natural human right hand, five fingers, fingers slightly curled, soft natural skin, matching the model's left-hand skin tone and lighting from upper left, against the unchanged sleeve cuff."
- Negative prompt: "six fingers, extra fingers, fused fingers, malformed hand, extra thumb, missing finger, distorted wrist, blurry, plastic skin."
- Sampler: DPM++ 2M Karras; CFG 5; steps 30.
- Seed strategy: batch 6 seeds at denoise 0.65; lock best for denoise sweep; lock best again for sampler swap if needed.
- Seam policy: 4-pixel overlap strip refined at denoise 0.2 around the cuff edge; histogram-match the hand to the model's other hand's swatch before seam refinement.
- Iteration ladder: mask audit -> denoise sweep -> prompt refinement (likely "fingers slightly curled" needed tightening) -> seam pass -> face/product composite-restore via masked paste-back.
- Composite preservation: face and product pasted back from original at the end with a 2-pixel alpha feather; backdrop accepted soft-preserve.
- Audit: pixel diff zero on face and product regions; perceptual similarity > 0.97 on backdrop; finger count == 5.

**Output excerpt:** the markdown plan plus a JSON object whose `mask` describes source / coverage / feather / polarity, whose `inpaint_type` is `latent_inpaint_trained`, whose `denoise` records default + sweep, whose `seam_policy` enumerates the overlap strip and histogram match, whose `audit` enumerates the pass/fail metrics.

## Limitations

- The skill plans inpainting and outpainting; it does not run the generator, paint masks, or verify outputs.
- Mask quality dominates outcomes; a well-planned workflow with a bad mask will produce a bad result. The plan flags mask audit as Step 1 but cannot enforce craft.
- Identity preservation through inpainting alone is fragile; for strict identity preservation the plan recommends pixel paste-back outside the diffusion path.
- Outpaint with very large extensions (more than 50% of the original area in a single pass) is unreliable; the plan recommends progressive strips.
- Inpaint-trained checkpoints are not uniformly available across all model families; for families lacking one, the plan falls back to latent inpaint with a base model plus mask blending, with explicit quality caveats.
- The skill refuses workflows whose intent is to misrepresent reality (forensic-style alteration of evidence images), to impersonate a real person, or to remove watermarks or attribution from third-party assets.
- The skill does not address the copyright status of training data or generated outputs in any jurisdiction; consult counsel for commercial use.
