## When to use

This skill produces methodology guidance for bioinformatics workflows. Outputs are not validated clinical or research conclusions and must be reviewed by qualified scientific staff before being acted upon. Pipelines built from this guidance must be wet-lab-validated and version-locked before any decisions are made on the results.

Use this skill when a team needs to plan a spatial transcriptomics analysis end-to-end and the choice between platforms has already been made. Specifically:

- A group has Visium, Visium HD, Xenium, MERFISH, CosMx, GeoMx, Slide-seq, or Stereo-seq data and needs to go from raw output to figures and tables.
- A lab is integrating spatial transcriptomics with a companion scRNA-seq reference or with multiplexed protein imaging.
- A core facility is bringing up a spatial service and wants the analysis stack laid out before the first production cohort.
- A pathology-driven project needs cell-type and niche-level outputs registered to histology that pathologists can read.

This skill does not pick the platform, the tissue, the section thickness, or the panel design — those decisions are upstream. It does not generate executable code; pair with the bioinformatics pipeline architect skill for workflow scaffolding. It assumes the team has at least one computational biologist who can implement the chosen tools.

## How to apply

Work the steps in order. The biggest determinant of methodology is whether the platform produces per-cell data (Xenium, MERFISH, CosMx, segmented Visium HD) or aggregate per-spot data (classic Visium, Slide-seq, GeoMx). Choose the analysis stack accordingly.

### 1. Identify the data resolution and what it implies

The first branch is resolution.

- **Per-cell platforms (Xenium, MERFISH, CosMx, segmented Visium HD).** Each transcript is associated with a cell after segmentation, and downstream analysis is single-cell-with-coordinates. Cell typing is direct; deconvolution is unnecessary.
- **Per-spot platforms (classic Visium, Slide-seq, GeoMx ROI).** Each measurement is a mixture of several cells. Deconvolution against a reference is the central computational task; cell-typing is indirect.
- **Imaging-based panel platforms (Xenium, MERFISH, CosMx).** Gene panel is fixed (hundreds to a few thousand targets); analysis must respect the panel and downstream "unbiased differential expression" claims are constrained.
- **Sequencing-based platforms (Visium family, Slide-seq, Stereo-seq).** Whole transcriptome or close to it; depth per spot or per bead is the limiting factor.

Write down the resolution and the panel scope. Every later analysis decision falls out of these two facts.

### 2. Run the platform's primary processing chain

Use the vendor or community-validated primary processor as the entry point. Do not write a parser for raw outputs; the validated tool exists and treats edge cases the right way.

- **Visium and Visium HD.** Vendor Space Ranger is the standard for FASTQ-to-spatial-object conversion (filtered count matrix, spot coordinates, low- and high-resolution images, JSON metadata). nf-core/spatialvi wraps this and the early downstream steps for cohort-scale processing.
- **Xenium.** The Xenium Ranger / Xenium Analyzer outputs include per-cell transcript counts, per-transcript coordinates, and segmentation masks; open-source ingest into AnnData/SpatialData is the typical handoff.
- **MERFISH and CosMx.** Vendor outputs (MERSCOPE, AtoMx) provide per-cell and per-transcript files; ingest into the scverse SpatialData/AnnData object model is the typical handoff.
- **Slide-seq, Stereo-seq.** Community tooling handles ingest; check whether the project uses scverse, Bioconductor (SpatialExperiment), or Seurat for the downstream pivot point.

The exit of this stage is a spatially-aware data object — AnnData with `.obsm['spatial']`, SpatialData, or a SpatialExperiment — plus the histology image at one or more resolutions.

### 3. Lock the data object and the toolkit ecosystem

Pick the toolkit ecosystem and commit. Trying to use scanpy/squidpy and Seurat/Bioconductor on the same dataset costs more than the modest benefits of orthogonality.

- **Python / scverse.** scanpy for general single-cell handling, squidpy for spatially-aware analysis, SpatialData/spatialdata-io for the unified object model, ehrapy or related for image handling. Strongest support for Visium, Visium HD, Xenium, MERFISH, CosMx out of the box.
- **R / Bioconductor.** SpatialExperiment for the object model, Seurat for general analysis, semla and related packages for image handling. Strong support for Visium and emerging support for cell-resolution platforms.

Pick one, document the choice, and write the pipeline against it. Mixed ecosystems are debt.

### 4. Per-sample QC with platform-appropriate metrics

Spatial-aware QC differs from non-spatial scRNA-seq QC.

For spot-based platforms:

- Per-spot total UMI count and number of detected genes, distribution and outliers.
- Per-spot mitochondrial fraction (where the panel includes mitochondrial genes).
- Spots in versus out of tissue, confirmed by image overlay.
- For HD-resolution Visium HD, also the binning level used in QC (8 µm, 16 µm, etc.).

For cell-resolution platforms:

- Per-cell transcript count distribution and segmentation-failure indicators.
- Per-cell area distribution; bimodal or right-tailed distributions can indicate segmentation artifacts (merged cells).
- Decoded versus rejected transcript counts and the rejection rate.
- Negative control codeword rate (built into MERFISH and Xenium codebooks).

Cohort-wise:

- Per-sample slide-level summary, with all samples viewable on one page.
- Distribution of cells per sample, transcripts per cell, and panel coverage.
- Batch-effect indicator: PCA on pseudobulk-per-sample colored by acquisition batch.

Define explicit thresholds for spot or cell inclusion and for sample exclusion. Document what happens to failed samples (re-section, exclude, flag).

### 5. Align to histology when histology matters

For sample types where histology is the reference frame (tumor margins, anatomical regions, brain atlases), the alignment is not optional.

- Vendor pipelines produce a registered image; verify the alignment visually before downstream domain inference.
- For multi-modal alignment (spatial transcriptomics plus H&E plus IHC plus IF), use a dedicated registration tool that supports affine plus non-rigid components.
- Pathologist-drawn annotations (tumor, stroma, immune-rich regions) should be ingested as additional `.obs` columns on the spots or cells, not maintained in a separate file.

A registered image, a transcript-coordinate alignment, and (optionally) pathologist annotations together form the spatial reference frame downstream tools consume.

### 6. Normalize and reduce dimensions

The first computational step on the spatial data is single-cell-style normalization:

- Library-size normalization and log-transform are the workhorse defaults; SCTransform or analytic Pearson residuals are alternatives.
- Select highly variable genes (or, for panel platforms, use all panel genes — variability selection within a small panel rarely helps).
- PCA, then a neighborhood graph (KNN on PCA), then UMAP for visualization and Leiden clustering for an initial expression-only grouping.

This produces a non-spatial clustering as a baseline. Compare it later against the spatially-aware clusterings; the differences are biologically meaningful.

### 7. Cell typing — direct for cell platforms, deconvolution for spot platforms

For **cell-resolution platforms**:

- Reference-based annotation against a matched scRNA-seq reference (Symphony, scANVI, SingleR-style) is the default.
- Manual annotation by marker genes is also widely used when the panel is well-known.
- Confidence scores per cell matter; carry them into downstream analyses and consider an "unassigned" bucket rather than forcing every cell into a type.

For **spot-based platforms**:

- Deconvolution against a matched scRNA-seq reference produces per-spot cell-type proportions. Cell2location, RCTD, Stereoscope, SPOTlight, and DSTG are common choices; each has different assumptions and computational profiles.
- Choose a deconvolution method based on the reference quality, the cohort size, and the computational budget. Probabilistic Bayesian methods (cell2location) are more expensive but produce uncertainty estimates; matrix-factorization methods are faster.
- For Visium HD at small bin sizes, the line between deconvolution and direct cell-typing blurs; consider per-bin cell-typing as an alternative if the resolution allows.

Document the reference dataset used for deconvolution or annotation. The reference is part of the analysis, not a hidden dependency.

### 8. Tissue-domain inference uses spatial information

A defining capability of spatial analysis is identifying tissue domains — regions of similar transcriptional state that are spatially contiguous. Approaches:

- **Spatially-aware clustering.** BayesSpace, STAGATE, SpaGCN, GraphST, and similar incorporate spatial neighborhood into the clustering objective. Output is per-spot or per-cell domain assignments that respect tissue architecture.
- **Pathology-driven domain definition.** Where pathologist annotations exist, use them directly; the algorithmic step is then post-hoc characterization of the annotated domains.
- **Joint domain and cell-type inference.** Some methods (cell2location with niche analysis, ROSE-style approaches) combine the two; useful when both are of interest.

Validate domain assignments against histology and against the non-spatial clustering. Where they disagree, the disagreements are usually biologically informative.

### 9. Neighborhood and cell-cell interaction analysis

Spatial data uniquely supports neighborhood analyses:

- **Neighborhood enrichment.** For each pair of cell types or domains, test whether they are spatial neighbors more or less often than expected by chance. squidpy and equivalents implement permutation-based versions.
- **Co-occurrence and clustering tendencies.** Per cell type, how often does it co-localize with others at varying spatial scales.
- **Ligand-receptor and communication analysis.** Tools like CellPhoneDB, CellChat, NICHES, and CellNeighborEX adapted for spatial contexts identify likely signaling pairs. For cell-resolution platforms with limited panels, panel coverage of the L-R database is the binding constraint.
- **Gradients and trajectories in space.** For tissue regions with clear axes (cortical depth, vessel-to-distant), spatial-aware trajectory analysis (Spateo, MISTy-style) is useful.

Decide what subset of these is in scope per the question. Running everything is rarely the right answer.

### 10. Differential expression in space

DE comes in several flavors in spatial data:

- **Between conditions, within domain or cell type.** Standard differential expression on pseudobulked or aggregated counts, with the sample as the replicate unit. Mixed models with patient-level random effects where applicable. Do not test per-spot or per-cell as the replicate unit — pseudoreplication is the default failure mode.
- **Between domains, within sample.** Marker identification per domain. Standard scanpy or Seurat-style marker tests.
- **Spatially variable genes.** Identify genes whose expression varies non-randomly across the tissue, regardless of clustering. Moran's I, Geary's C, SpatialDE, or SPARK approaches.
- **Differential abundance across conditions.** Are certain cell types or domains enriched in disease versus control. Tools like miloR, scCODA, or simple pseudobulk-with-replicates depending on cohort size.

Pre-register the contrasts when the cohort is small; data-driven comparisons in small cohorts inflate false positives.

### 11. Integrate companion modalities deliberately

Common companion data:

- **Matched scRNA-seq.** Used as the deconvolution reference and as a typing scaffold. The reference must include the cell types actually present in the tissue; missing types corrupt deconvolution.
- **Multiplexed protein imaging (IMC, CODEX, multiplexed IHC).** Spatially-resolved protein measurements complement transcript-resolved data. Co-registration is the technical challenge; downstream comparison is per-region.
- **Bulk RNA-seq of the same samples.** Useful as a deconvolution sanity check (pseudobulk of spatial should approximate bulk).
- **Pathology annotations and clinical metadata.** Per-sample covariates feed differential analyses and stratifications.

Document the integration approach per modality. "We have an scRNA-seq reference" is not a plan; "we use atlas X with cell types Y for cell2location-based deconvolution with location-specific priors" is.

### 12. Plan for cohort-level analysis, not single-slide demos

Most published methods are demonstrated on one slide. Real cohorts have 10-100+ slides and force decisions:

- **Sample integration.** Across slides, simple per-slide normalization plus PCA usually carries batch with it. Use Harmony, scVI, or scANVI as appropriate for cohort integration before clustering.
- **Slide-level versus patient-level replication.** Multiple slides per patient is the right design where feasible; counted as technical replicates within patient.
- **Compute scaling.** Some methods (cell2location, certain spatially-aware clusterings) become expensive at cohort scale. Plan the compute budget per method up front.

A 50-slide cohort is not a 50x version of a 1-slide analysis. The architecture changes.

### 13. Reporting for the audience

Pick the figures and tables that match the consumer:

- **For pathology teams.** Domain maps overlaid on histology; per-slide summary panels; gene-of-interest overlays at request.
- **For computational biologists.** Standard UMAP plus spatial maps; the deconvolution proportions; differential tables and pathway enrichment.
- **For target discovery.** Cell-type-by-domain marker tables; ligand-receptor candidate lists with literature filters; expression in disease-versus-control with effect sizes.
- **For atlas publication.** Reproducible notebooks, deposited data, and a registered analysis version with code references.

The audience determines what gets published and how. Decide before generating figures.

### 14. Provenance and reproducibility specifics

Spatial pipelines have specific provenance items beyond a generic sequencing pipeline:

- The vendor pipeline version (Space Ranger, Xenium Analyzer) and the parameters used.
- The reference transcriptome, the gene panel (for panel platforms), and the codebook version (for MERFISH/CosMx).
- The segmentation parameters and the segmentation tool version where applicable.
- The scRNA-seq reference dataset version used for deconvolution.
- The deconvolution method, hyperparameters, and posterior samples or seed for reproducibility.
- The histology image registration parameters and any pathologist-annotation snapshots.

Treat the analysis object (AnnData, SpatialData, Seurat object) as the artifact; checksum it on completion and link to it from the report.

### 15. Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Treating Visium spots as cells.** A spot covers multiple cells. Treating them as single cells corrupts both expression analysis and biological interpretation.
- **Running deconvolution against an unmatched reference.** A reference from a different tissue or developmental stage will mis-deconvolve. Match the reference.
- **Per-spot or per-cell differential testing across conditions.** Without sample-level aggregation or mixed models, the effective sample size is the number of spots, not the number of patients. Inflated p-values follow.
- **Ignoring panel limitations on imaging platforms.** Conclusions about pathway activity require panel coverage of that pathway. State the limitation in the report.
- **Single-slide methods extended to cohorts without re-architecting.** Compute and integration changes are real.
- **Failing to validate against histology.** Spatial methods that disagree with pathology are wrong more often than pathology is wrong; always cross-check.
- **Throwing away unassigned cells.** "Unassigned" is a real category, especially on panel platforms. Forced assignment misleads.

### 16. The final shape of the deliverable

A complete spatial transcriptomics analysis plan from this skill includes:

- The primary-processing path and the data-object format the analysis lives in.
- The QC metrics and acceptance gates.
- The cell-typing or deconvolution approach with the reference dataset.
- The tissue-domain inference approach.
- The neighborhood and differential analyses in scope.
- The companion-modality integration plan where applicable.
- The cohort integration approach.
- The reporting plan tailored to the consumer audience.
- The provenance fields the pipeline records.

## Examples

**Example A — Visium HD colorectal cancer cohort with matched scRNA-seq.** Eighteen Visium HD slides across nine patients (paired tumor and adjacent normal). The plan is: Space Ranger primary processing; ingest into SpatialData/AnnData; QC with per-spot total UMIs, mitochondrial fraction, and in-tissue confirmation; cell2location deconvolution against a published colorectal scRNA-seq atlas, with patient-specific normalization; BayesSpace-style domain inference per slide and Harmony-integrated cross-cohort clustering; pseudobulk-per-sample differential expression for tumor versus normal with patient as a covariate; neighborhood enrichment for tumor-microenvironment cell pairs of interest; a pathology-overlay report per slide and an aggregate report per patient. cell2location runtime is budgeted explicitly given the slide count.

**Example B — Xenium healthy lung cohort.** Three donors with two slides each, a 380-gene curated lung panel. The plan is: vendor Xenium analyzer for primary outputs; ingest per-cell counts and transcript coordinates into AnnData; QC with per-cell transcript count, segmentation-area distribution, and negative-codeword rate; reference-based cell typing against a lung scRNA-seq atlas (scANVI or Symphony); per-donor and pooled UMAPs and spatial maps; squidpy neighborhood enrichment for epithelial-immune cell-type pairs; airway-axis depth gradient analysis using vessel and airway annotations from co-stained H&E; a manuscript-ready figure set including per-donor cell-type fractions and neighborhood scores. Panel limitations are flagged: pathway activity inferences are restricted to panel-covered pathways.

**Example C — MERFISH brain-section study.** A single coronal mouse brain section with a 500-gene panel. The plan is: vendor primary outputs ingested into SpatialData; QC with per-cell transcript count and blank-codeword rate; reference-based cell typing against the Allen Brain Atlas reference adapted to the panel; pathology-region overlay (cortical layers, hippocampus, etc.) imported as annotations; per-region cell-type composition; spatially-variable gene identification with Moran's I; output is a notebook plus a figure set for a method-paper supplementary section.

## Limitations

- This skill does not generate executable code. Pair with the bioinformatics pipeline architect skill for the workflow scaffolding.
- The named tools reflect current defaults; verify currency at implementation time and substitute when newer methods are well-validated.
- Vendor primary processors (Space Ranger, Xenium Analyzer) are not fully open source; their inclusion is unavoidable but their outputs are open-format and the downstream stack is fully community-tooled.
- Whole-transcriptome at single-cell resolution (Stereo-seq, certain Visium HD configurations) is a moving target; treat the guidance here as a starting frame and revisit per project.
- Multi-modal spatial workflows (transcriptomics plus protein plus epigenomics on the same section) are emerging and not covered in depth.
