# Support Reply Drafter

## When to use

Reach for this skill the moment triage is done and a real human-quality reply needs to leave the queue. The customer has been categorized, prioritized, and routed; now the question is what to actually say. This is the second hand-off in the support workflow, not the first — if the message is unsorted, run a triage skill first and come back.

Use it especially when the reply is non-trivial: an escalation that needs both empathy and firmness, a decline that has to be gracious, a partial resolution that has to be honest about its limits, an apology that has to acknowledge fault without inviting unbounded liability, or a status update on a multi-day investigation where the temptation is to send "no news" and the right move is to send "here is exactly what we know and what we are doing next."

Skip the skill for two-line factual replies a saved-response macro already covers, for purely internal-routing notes that never leave the help-desk, and for first-response acknowledgments where intake-triage already produced the draft. Reaching for a model on a reply that should be a canned response is wasted effort and tends to over-bake the writing.

## How to apply

Drafting a support reply is not "be empathetic and helpful" in the abstract — it is a small number of high-leverage decisions stacked in a specific order. Follow them.

1. **Read the latest customer message twice.** Once for content, once for emotional register. Note where the customer is anchored: are they asking for help, asking for accountability, or asking for compensation? These three asks need different reply structures and getting them mixed up is the most common drafting mistake.

2. **Read the prior thread.** Skim earlier turns for two things: facts already established (do not re-ask), and promises already made (do not contradict). A reply that asks for an order number that was given in turn two is a relationship-damage event the customer will remember longer than the underlying issue.

3. **Lock the outcome.** The `outcome` input is load-bearing. Each value implies a different reply spine:
   - **apologize** — own the fault plainly, name the impact, state the remediation, close. Do not couple an apology with a defensive explanation; if both are needed, send the apology first and the explanation in a follow-up.
   - **explain** — acknowledge briefly, then explain causally, then state what changes (if anything) so this is less likely next time. No apology unless an apology is also warranted.
   - **resolve** — confirm the problem, state the fix, give the customer the smallest sufficient instruction set, offer the next step if it doesn't work.
   - **partial-resolve** — name what you can do and what you cannot, in that order. Resist the temptation to bury the "cannot" in the middle paragraph.
   - **escalate** — explain that the issue is being handed to a specific named team or owner, what they will do, when the customer will hear back. Do not escalate silently; the customer must know.
   - **decline** — say no clearly, give one honest reason (not three), and offer the closest alternative you can in good faith.
   - **gather-info** — ask the minimum number of questions to unblock the next step, framed in plain language with examples. Bulleted lists are okay; interrogation lists of fifteen items are not.
   - **hold** — acknowledge that you are still working on it, share what you have learned or ruled out so the customer feels progress, and set a concrete next-update time.

4. **Calibrate to relationship tier.** Tier changes three things: length, formality, and concession latitude.
   - Trial and free: short, warm, concession latitude limited to documentation pointers and goodwill.
   - SMB: medium-length, warm, concession latitude includes credits up to one billing cycle of MRR (defer larger to a human).
   - Mid-market and enterprise: longer, more formal, names a specific owner, references a known account context if any was provided, concession latitude includes credit notes and structured remediation plans.
   - Strategic: treat as enterprise plus — assume the reply will be forwarded to a procurement contact and/or executive sponsor. No internal jargon, no implicit assumptions about product knowledge.

   Tier does not change empathy. A free-tier user in genuine distress still gets careful prose; an enterprise CISO with a casual question does not need a six-paragraph response.

5. **Apply the voice.** If `brand_voice` was provided, treat the descriptors as constraints on the draft. If `prior_replies` were provided, match register and cadence — not vocabulary, register. The most common failure mode of LLM-drafted replies is what experienced writers call "thesaurus voice": correct register but unnatural word choice. Read the draft sentence by sentence and ask "would a person actually say it this way." If no, rewrite.

6. **Draft the spine first.** Write the load-bearing sentences before the connective tissue: the opener, the central claim or commitment, the close. If those three sentences are right, the paragraph around them almost writes itself. If they are wrong, no amount of polish will fix the reply.

7. **Opener rules.**
   - Never lead with "Thank you for reaching out to support." It is a tax on the customer's attention and signals scripted reply.
   - Address by name if a name was extractable.
   - First sentence does one of: acknowledges the situation in the customer's own framing, validates the inconvenience without grovelling, or — if the outcome is `resolve` — leads with the answer.
   - For `apologize`, the apology lives in the first sentence, not the second.

8. **Middle rules.**
   - One idea per paragraph. Paragraphs over four sentences should be split.
   - Use plain language for plain things and precise language for technical things. Do not soften technical specifics to make them more "friendly" — customers asking technical questions read that as condescension.
   - When stating facts, state facts. When stating opinions or apologies, mark them as such ("I'm sorry that…", not "It is unfortunate that…").
   - Bulleted lists are appropriate for: sequential steps, enumerated options, and questions in a `gather-info` reply. They are inappropriate for tone-bearing paragraphs.

9. **Close rules.**
   - Name the next step. Either who is doing what by when, or what you need from the customer to proceed.
   - Do not close with hollow phrases like "let me know if you have any other questions." Replace with a specific, useful close: "If the workaround doesn't take, just reply and I'll pull a teammate in," or "I'll check back tomorrow with an update either way."
   - Sign with a real name or, at minimum, the team name. Avoid "The Support Team" with no further specificity unless the company's voice guide explicitly mandates it.

10. **Honesty discipline.** Several anti-patterns to refuse to produce, regardless of the outcome:
    - Do not claim "we are looking into it" when the facts say nobody has been assigned yet. Say "I am pulling in a teammate now."
    - Do not promise a fix timeline that has not been committed by engineering. State a next-update time instead.
    - Do not use the phrase "in the unlikely event" or other minimizers when the event has, in fact, already occurred for this customer.
    - Do not blame third parties to deflect when the customer's contract is with us. We can describe a dependency without dodging accountability.
    - Do not invent product features or behaviors. If unsure, say "let me confirm and come back to you."

11. **Apology calibration.** A real apology has three components: ownership ("we got this wrong"), specificity ("the export silently failed for paying customers on Chrome 129+ from Tuesday morning"), and remediation ("we shipped a fix at 14:20 UTC and are crediting affected accounts for the day"). Anything labeled "apology" that has fewer than two of these components is performative and the customer will read it as such.

12. **Decline calibration.** A clean decline has three components: a clear no, one honest reason, and the closest in-good-faith alternative. Avoid hedging that reads as "maybe, eventually, who knows" — customers prefer a firm no over a soft maybe. If the answer is genuinely "not now but plausibly later," say "not on the current roadmap; I'll log your case for the next planning cycle" rather than implying commitment.

13. **Escalation calibration.** Never escalate silently. The reply must say: who is now handling it (by name or role), what they will do as the next step, and when the customer will hear back. If "when" is uncertain, give a worst-case bound ("by Friday end-of-day at the latest") rather than an open-ended promise.

14. **Length discipline.** Most replies are too long. Quick check: read the draft and delete every sentence whose removal does not change the customer-facing meaning. If the reply is still over 200 words for a non-enterprise tier, consider whether you are over-explaining. Enterprise replies may legitimately run 300+ words; consumer replies almost never should.

15. **Sensitivity flags.** Before finalizing, scan for:
    - Legal-adjacent language (lawsuit, attorney, GDPR, subpoena) — do not draft substantive responses to these; emit a brief acknowledgment and route to legal in the internal note.
    - Self-harm or harm-to-others language — do not draft a normal support reply; emit a brief, warm acknowledgment and route to a senior human.
    - Refund or credit dollar figures that exceed the agent's stated concession latitude — propose the maximum within latitude and flag the gap for human approval rather than drafting the larger amount.

16. **Produce alternates for the spine.** Output the chosen draft plus one or two phrasing alternates for the opener, the central commitment, and the close. The human can swap. This is much higher value than producing two whole alternate drafts, which the human will not actually read.

17. **Write the "why this draft" note.** Three to six bullets covering: the outcome chosen, the tier assumption, the load-bearing facts pulled from `facts`, anything explicitly declined ("did not offer a refund because facts indicate the customer is past the 30-day window"), and any flagged sensitivities the human must verify before sending. This is not customer-facing.

18. **Final read.** Read the draft aloud (or in your head with that cadence). If any sentence feels like it would land awkwardly when said by a human, rewrite. If the close trails off, replace it. If the apology sounds like a marketing email, redraft.

## Inputs

- `ticket_thread` (required) — full thread or at minimum the latest customer message plus enough context to avoid repeating questions or contradicting prior promises.
- `outcome` (required, choice) — what the reply is doing. Drives structure.
- `relationship_tier` (optional, choice) — calibrates length, formality, and concession latitude.
- `facts` (optional, text) — authoritative internal notes. Treated as ground truth; informs the draft but is never echoed verbatim to the customer.
- `brand_voice` (optional, text) — tone descriptors or short voice guide.
- `prior_replies` (optional, text) — one or two example on-brand replies for register matching.

## Outputs

- `reply_draft` (markdown) — the send-ready reply with optional subject line, plus an internal "Why this draft" note (clearly separated, customer must never see it).
- `alternates` (JSON) — phrasing alternates for opener, central commitment, and close, so a human reviewer can swap a sentence without redrafting the whole reply.

## Examples

### Example 1 — partial-resolve with credit (SMB tier)

**Input ticket_thread (latest message):**

> Your team finally responded after 18 hours about my failed exports. By that point I had already manually rebuilt the report in a spreadsheet for my board. I'd like the rest of this month refunded.

**Input outcome:** `partial-resolve`
**Input relationship_tier:** `smb`
**Input facts:** "Customer is on $199/mo Growth plan. Outage lasted 4 hours, not 18. SLA breached on first response (responded at hour 5). Concession latitude: up to one full billing cycle for confirmed SLA breach. Eng has shipped fix and added monitoring."

**Output reply_draft (excerpt):**

> Hi —
>
> I'm sorry. We missed our first-response SLA on your report, and I understand the export was directly in the path of your board prep — that compounded the cost in a way our SLA numbers don't reflect.
>
> Here's what I can do, and what I can't. I'm applying a full credit for this month's Growth plan to your account; you'll see it on next month's invoice. On the 18-hour figure: our records show our first response went out at hour 5, which is still a clear SLA miss but not the timeline you experienced. If you have the email thread on hand I'd like to look at it directly — there may be a routing problem on our side that swallowed earlier replies.
>
> The export bug itself was fixed Tuesday and we've added monitoring so a silent failure of that kind should page us, not surprise you next time.
>
> If a full refund of the month rather than a credit would work better for your accounting, I can route that for approval — let me know.
>
> — Sam, Support

**Internal "Why this draft" note (not for customer):**
- outcome = partial-resolve; opened with apology since SLA was genuinely breached, but separated the fault we own (response time) from the figure I cannot confirm (18h).
- tier = smb; offered full month credit (within latitude); explicitly flagged that a refund-in-cash version requires human approval.
- did not draft an over-apology for the outage itself since duration was 4h, which is within standard P2 SLA.

### Example 2 — decline a feature request (enterprise tier)

**Input ticket_thread (latest message):**

> We've been on your platform for two years. Could you add a way to set custom retention rules per workspace? Without it we may need to evaluate alternatives at renewal.

**Input outcome:** `decline`
**Input relationship_tier:** `enterprise`
**Input facts:** "Retention rules per workspace not on roadmap; engineering has assessed and considers it out of scope until 2027 at earliest. CSM is Jordan. Customer is $80k ARR. Account in good standing."

**Output reply_draft (excerpt):**

> Hi —
>
> Thanks for raising this directly — and for the candor about the renewal context. I want to be straight with you: per-workspace retention rules aren't on our roadmap for this year, and engineering has assessed the work as out of scope until 2027 at the earliest. I'd rather tell you that now than imply movement we can't deliver.
>
> What we can offer in the near term: org-wide retention policies (already shipped), plus workspace-level export automation that some customers have used to approximate per-workspace retention through their own pipeline. I'm happy to walk Jordan and your team through the second one if it's worth a session.
>
> If retention granularity is renewal-blocking, I'd like to get that on the record with Jordan and your account team before we get close to the date, so we can have an honest conversation about fit rather than a surprise one.
>
> — Sam, Support

### Example 3 — escalation (mid-market, security-flavored)

**Input outcome:** `escalate`
**Input relationship_tier:** `mid-market`
**Input facts:** "Customer reports unauthorized password change. Trust & Safety lead is Priya, will own the case. Initial response SLA on T&S tickets is 30 minutes."

**Output reply_draft (excerpt):**

> Hi —
>
> Thank you for flagging this — an unexpected password change is exactly the kind of signal we treat as a security incident from the first message, not after investigation. I've pulled in Priya on our Trust & Safety team to take this directly; she'll reach out within the next 30 minutes with the next steps to verify your identity, re-secure the account, and start the audit trail check.
>
> In the meantime: do not click any password reset links from emails you didn't initiate, even if they look like ours. If you spot any other unusual activity, just reply here and it'll route to Priya.
>
> — Sam, Support

## Limitations

- Output quality depends heavily on the truthfulness and completeness of `facts`. Garbage facts in, plausible-sounding falsehoods out. If `facts` is empty, the agent should bias toward "let me confirm and come back" rather than confabulate.
- Tier-based concession latitude is a heuristic, not policy. Real companies have specific dollar thresholds, approval flows, and contractual SLA credits. Treat the latitude described in step 4 as defaults to be overridden by the team's actual policy.
- The skill drafts in the language of the input thread. If the customer wrote in a language the agent cannot draft natively in (with confidence), it falls back to English and flags the language for human follow-up.
- Voice matching is approximate. With no `brand_voice` or `prior_replies`, the default voice is warm-but-professional and intentionally generic; teams with a distinctive house style should supply at least two prior replies as anchors.
- The skill does not send. It produces a draft for human review. Any workflow that auto-sends the output without a human pass is using the skill outside its intended scope.
- Legal-adjacent and harm-related messages are intentionally not handled at full draft depth. The skill will produce a holding acknowledgment and flag for routing rather than attempt to write a substantive response to a subpoena, GDPR request, or self-harm disclosure.
