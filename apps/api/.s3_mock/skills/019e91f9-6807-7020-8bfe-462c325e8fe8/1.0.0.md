# Reconciliation Builder

## When to use

Use this skill when an accountant or controller needs to design or strengthen the reconciliation process for a balance-sheet account: cash, accounts receivable, accounts payable, prepaid expenses, accrued liabilities, inventory, fixed assets, intercompany, deferred revenue, or any other account whose GL balance should tie to an independent source. The skill takes an account name, the external source the GL ties to, the transaction volume, and the current pain — and produces a process specification plus a workpaper template that an external auditor will accept.

The skill is for the design moment, not the execution moment. The output is a process: who owns it, what cadence, what evidence, what matching rules separate "matched" from "exception," how exceptions are aged, who reviews and signs off, and what triggers escalation. The skill also produces a template workpaper so that every execution of the rec produces a consistent, defensible artifact rather than a one-off spreadsheet.

It applies whether the user is designing a new rec for a brand-new account (a freshly opened bank account, a newly created accrual line), tightening a rec that the auditor flagged as weak, automating a rec that has grown beyond what a spreadsheet can support, or unwinding aged reconciling items that have accumulated for months.

Do not use this skill to perform the rec itself for a given period; this is the design skill. The execution lives in whatever tool the workpaper is filled in with — a spreadsheet, a rec automation platform, or an in-ERP rec module. Do not use it to design the journal entries that the rec produces; entries belong to the `journal-entry-author` skill, which the rec process feeds into.

The skill is opinionated about three things. First: a rec is not "the GL agrees to itself." A rec must compare the GL to a source that is independent of the GL — otherwise you have proven nothing. Second: every reconciling item must be aged, owned, and have a closure plan. An aged reconciling item with no plan is the most common audit finding. Third: review is a separate role from preparation; without two-eyes, the rec is a self-attestation, which auditors discount.

## How to apply

The skill runs a deterministic eight-stage pipeline. Each stage adds a layer to the reconciliation design.

### Stage 1 — Classify the account family

1. Map the account to one of the standard rec families. The family determines the rec shape:
   - **Cash and bank accounts** — GL to bank statement. High volume, fast-clearing items, timing-difference dominant.
   - **Accounts receivable** — GL to AR sub-ledger or aging. Sub-ledger detail must roll to control account.
   - **Accounts payable** — GL to AP sub-ledger or aging. Same shape as AR.
   - **Prepaid expenses** — GL to amortization schedule. Schedule-based, low volume per account, multiple prepaid items often grouped.
   - **Accrued liabilities** — GL to supporting estimates or schedules. The hardest family because the "external source" is itself an internal estimate; the rec is between two internal calculations.
   - **Fixed assets / intangibles** — GL to fixed asset register, including roll-forward of additions, disposals, depreciation, impairment.
   - **Inventory** — GL to physical inventory count or perpetual inventory system, plus adjustments.
   - **Deferred revenue / contract liabilities** — GL to revenue recognition schedule. Sub-ledger comparable in mature systems.
   - **Intercompany** — GL of entity A's intercompany account to GL of entity B's mirror account, FX-adjusted.
   - **Equity** — GL to cap table or stock plan administrator records.
   - **Income tax** — GL to tax provision workpaper.
   - **Suspense and clearing** — should balance to zero or to known items; should not exist as a permanent residence for unknowns.
2. The family choice determines which of the patterns in this skill's pattern library applies.

### Stage 2 — Establish the independent source

3. Identify the source the GL will be reconciled to. The source must be independent: produced or held by a party other than the GL preparer. For cash, that means the bank. For AR, the sub-ledger if separately maintained, or the customer's confirmation for a confirmation-based control. For prepaid, the original invoice plus the amortization schedule. For accrued liabilities, the underlying calculation (estimate, schedule, third-party data).
4. Note that "independent" is a continuum. Bank statements are highly independent (third party, contractually accurate). Sub-ledgers maintained by the same accountant as the GL are partially independent (different system, same human). Internal estimates are barely independent (different spreadsheet, same author). Flag the independence tier explicitly so review knows how skeptical to be.
5. For accounts where independence is weak (accrued liabilities, deferred revenue under complex models), compensating controls are needed: review by a second person, comparison to prior period with explanation of changes, comparison to a different basis (e.g., reconciling accrued bonus to plan-document calculations).
6. Define the data extraction process for the source. How and when is the data pulled? In what format? Where is the extracted file stored? Document this so the next cycle does not improvise.

### Stage 3 — Define cadence and timing

7. Default cadences by family:
   - Cash: daily for high-volume operating accounts; monthly for low-volume reserve accounts.
   - AR / AP: monthly aligned with the close.
   - Prepaid / accrued: monthly with the close.
   - Fixed assets: monthly roll-forward; full physical inventory annually.
   - Inventory: monthly cycle count plus annual physical for product inventory; perpetual reconciliation continuously.
   - Intercompany: monthly close; ideally daily soft-match in companies with high intercompany volume.
   - Equity: quarterly with a monthly check.
   - Tax: quarterly with a year-end true-up.
8. Anchor the cadence to the close. The monthly rec runs in Phase 3 of the close (per the close-conductor skill). Daily recs run continuously and contribute the period-end day's snapshot.
9. Define the cutoff date for each source. The GL period-end is one date; the bank statement period-end may be a different date; the sub-ledger snapshot is taken at a specific moment. If cutoff dates differ, the rec includes a reconciling-item line for items in between. Do not let cutoff mismatch silently absorb true differences.

### Stage 4 — Specify the matching rules

10. Matching is the core of the rec. Define the rule for declaring two items "matched":
    - Cash: exact amount and value date, plus a counterparty identifier if available. Use the bank's transaction reference as the join key.
    - AR / AP: invoice number is the join key; amount tie should be exact unless partial payments are expected, in which case match on remaining open balance per invoice.
    - Prepaid: each line in the schedule matches a specific invoice and amortizes over a known period.
    - Accrued: rec is a top-down comparison of total accrued to the estimate; no item-level match because accrued lines are aggregations.
    - Fixed assets: each asset's roll-forward (beginning balance + additions − disposals − depreciation = ending balance) is the rec, line by asset.
    - Intercompany: amounts in counterparty currency match within a tolerance (commonly $0); FX rate differences are computed against a published rate.
11. Define tolerances. A $0.01 difference on a cash rec is real and not a tolerance. A 0.5% rate-driven difference on an FX revaluation is expected. Set tolerances by source and document them; tolerances above the threshold escalate to investigation.
12. Define matching automation. For volumes below ~200 transactions per month, manual matching is acceptable. Above that, semi-automated tools (Excel formula match, lookup join) help. Above ~2,000 transactions, true matching engines with fuzzy rules or ML-assisted matching are warranted. The choice is a recommendation, not a mandate; many small companies run monthly recs by eye successfully.
13. For sub-ledger-to-GL reconciliations, the only join key needed is the period-end total. The sub-ledger detail proves itself by line; the GL proves itself by entry; only the totals need to match.

### Stage 5 — Define reconciling items

14. After matching, the unmatched items on each side are reconciling items. Categorize them:
    - **Timing differences** — legitimately in transit (outstanding checks, deposits in transit, in-flight wire). Resolve themselves in the next period. Owned by the rec preparer.
    - **GL errors** — wrong amount, wrong account, duplicate entry. Resolve by a correcting journal entry. Owned by the rec preparer, executed via the JE author skill.
    - **Source errors** — bank fee not on GL but legitimately charged, mis-keyed bank entry. Resolve via a correcting JE or a bank dispute.
    - **Unknown items** — neither side can identify the item. These age. They should not exist for long.
15. Every reconciling item has fields: id, date first seen, age (days since first seen), description, dollar amount, category, owner, target resolution date, status.
16. Aging buckets: 0-30 days, 31-60, 61-90, 90+. Aged items 60+ days are a control finding; 90+ are a material weakness candidate. The rec design includes an escalation rule: any reconciling item that crosses 60 days gets a manager review; any item that crosses 90 days gets a controller review and a write-off decision.
17. Define a write-off rule. An aged reconciling item that cannot be cleared is at some point written off. Default rule: items below $500 aged over 90 days are written off with controller approval; items above $500 require investigation and a memo before write-off. Write-offs themselves are journal entries and go through normal sign-off.

### Stage 6 — Build the workpaper template

18. The workpaper is what the auditor sees and what next month's preparer copies forward. Template structure:
    - **Header.** Account name and number, period, preparer, reviewer, prep date, review date, status (in-progress, prepared, reviewed, signed-off).
    - **Source data.** GL balance per period close; independent source balance per source cutoff; if cutoff dates differ, a small adjustment line for the difference.
    - **Reconciling items table.** All unmatched items with the fields from step 15. Aging is visible; aged items in red.
    - **Matched volume summary.** Count and dollar volume of matched items, as a sanity check.
    - **Variance.** GL balance minus source balance minus net reconciling items. Should equal zero. If non-zero, the rec is unfinished and cannot be signed off.
    - **JE references.** Any journal entries posted as a result of this rec (corrections, write-offs) referenced by id.
    - **Sign-offs.** Preparer initials and date; reviewer initials and date.
    - **Roll-forward.** A small section noting changes from prior period: prior period reconciling items still open, items added this period, items cleared.
19. The template is the same shape every month. Consistency lets the reviewer scan for differences from prior month, and lets aged items stand out.

### Stage 7 — Define review and sign-off

20. Preparer and reviewer must be different humans. The reviewer's job is not to redo the rec; it is to attest that the rec is well-formed: the source is appropriate, the matching rules were applied, the reconciling items have owners and ages, the variance is zero, the workpaper is complete.
21. Review checklist (the reviewer signs off attesting):
    - GL balance matches the period close report.
    - Source balance matches the source extract.
    - Cutoff differences are explained.
    - Reconciling items have owners and target resolution dates.
    - No items have aged beyond the escalation threshold without manager involvement.
    - Variance is zero.
    - Any related JEs have been posted with proper support.
22. Time-box the review: the reviewer commits to completing review within a defined window (default 24 hours of preparer hand-off) so the rec does not sit in queue and delay the close.
23. Material recs (large accounts, accounts with prior audit findings, accounts near year-end) get a second reviewer at the controller or CFO level. The design names which accounts qualify.

### Stage 8 — Exceptions queue and escalation

24. Define the exceptions queue: the central log of all reconciling items across all accounts. Each item has its account, age, owner, status. The queue is reviewed weekly by the controller; items aged past 30 days become a standing meeting agenda item.
25. Escalation thresholds:
    - 30 days: surfaced in weekly controller review; owner provides update.
    - 60 days: controller assigns a specific owner with a deadline; reasoning documented.
    - 90 days: escalated to CFO; written justification for keeping the item open or write-off decision required.
    - 180 days: item must be resolved or written off; an item still open at 180 days is, by definition, a material weakness.
26. Resolution is documented. When an item clears, the rec workpaper records what happened to it: matched to a later transaction, corrected via JE, written off via JE, identified as a source error and disputed with the third party. The closure note feeds the next audit's documentation.
27. Pattern detection: if the same account generates aged reconciling items every month, the underlying process is broken. The design surfaces the pattern and recommends a root-cause investigation rather than treating each item as a one-off.

## Reconciliation pattern library

The skill carries pattern designs for common account families. Each pattern instantiates with the user's specifics.

### Pattern R1 — Cash reconciliation

- Source: bank statement (formal) plus bank feed (continuous).
- Cadence: monthly (formal); daily (informal feed-based).
- Matching: exact-amount, value-date match; bank reference as join key.
- Reconciling-item categories: outstanding checks, deposits in transit, bank fees not yet booked, returned items, wires not yet recorded, error corrections.
- Aging: outstanding checks aged 180+ days are stale and written off (subject to escheatment rules); deposits in transit aged 5+ days are suspect.
- Special concerns: positive pay matching, ACH return windows, fraud-vector accounts (operating, payroll), inactive accounts that should be closed.

### Pattern R2 — Accounts receivable reconciliation

- Source: AR sub-ledger detail (sum of customer accounts).
- Cadence: monthly with close.
- Matching: sub-ledger total to GL control account; sub-ledger detail to invoices and credits.
- Reconciling-item categories: invoices recorded in GL but not in sub-ledger (or vice versa), unapplied cash, credit memos in flight, FX revaluation differences.
- Aging: AR aging itself is the secondary deliverable — sub-ledger reconciliation is to the control account; aging analysis is the basis for the bad-debt reserve.
- Special concerns: customer master changes mid-period, reclassifications between current/non-current, intercompany AR vs. external AR distinction.

### Pattern R3 — Accounts payable reconciliation

- Source: AP sub-ledger detail (sum of vendor accounts).
- Cadence: monthly with close.
- Matching: sub-ledger total to GL control account; sub-ledger detail to vendor invoices.
- Reconciling-item categories: invoices entered but not posted, payments in transit, vendor credits, voided checks, duplicate entries.
- Aging: AP aging supports the AP-accrual sizing in the close; the rec confirms sub-ledger integrity, not the accrual itself.
- Special concerns: 1099 vendor tracking, related-party AP, intercompany AP separated.

### Pattern R4 — Prepaid expenses

- Source: prepaid amortization schedule (one tab per prepaid item with payment date, total amount, period covered, monthly amortization, accumulated, remaining).
- Cadence: monthly.
- Matching: total of remaining balances on the schedule must equal the prepaid GL account; monthly amortization JE matches the schedule's monthly column.
- Reconciling-item categories: new prepaid not yet on the schedule, schedule errors (wrong period, wrong total), early termination of a prepaid contract.
- Aging: prepaid balances should decline over time as amortization runs; a balance that doesn't move month-over-month is suspect.
- Special concerns: prepaid items below materiality threshold may be expensed immediately; the schedule should not be cluttered with $200 prepaid items.

### Pattern R5 — Accrued liabilities

- Source: supporting estimate or calculation (commission accrual schedule, bonus accrual calculation, accrued legal estimate, payroll accrual).
- Cadence: monthly.
- Matching: GL balance to the supporting calculation; the reconciliation is essentially an attestation that the GL balance equals the calculation.
- Reconciling-item categories: rare; the rec usually resolves to zero by definition because the JE was sized to the calculation. Mismatch signals an error.
- Aging: stale accruals that don't reverse or pay out are a major audit concern. Aged accrued balances should be investigated and either paid, reversed, or re-estimated.
- Special concerns: independence is weak — the accrual is internal. Compensating controls: second-person review of the calculation, comparison to prior month, comparison to actual settlement when it occurs.

### Pattern R6 — Fixed assets

- Source: fixed asset register / sub-ledger.
- Cadence: monthly roll-forward; annual physical verification of a sample.
- Matching: register total to GL; per-asset roll-forward (BB + additions − disposals − depreciation = EB).
- Reconciling-item categories: additions in GL but not register (or vice versa), depreciation calculation errors, disposals not removed from register, impairment indicators not booked.
- Aging: assets idle for extended periods, assets fully depreciated but still on the books, assets disposed without proper retirement entry.
- Special concerns: capitalization threshold consistency, useful-life policy adherence, leasehold improvements vs. owned-asset distinction.

### Pattern R7 — Intercompany

- Source: counterparty entity's mirror account.
- Cadence: monthly; ideally daily soft-match continuously.
- Matching: amounts in counterparty currency match within a tolerance (usually $0); FX differences computed against a published rate snap.
- Reconciling-item categories: in-transit intercompany invoices, FX rate differences, timing differences for cross-period transactions.
- Aging: imbalances over 30 days are a flag; over 60 days are a finding.
- Special concerns: transfer-pricing implications, intercompany loan vs. trade balances separated, elimination journal accuracy in consolidation.

### Pattern R8 — Deferred revenue / contract liabilities

- Source: revenue recognition schedule (per contract, per performance obligation).
- Cadence: monthly.
- Matching: total deferred balance per schedules equals deferred revenue GL balance.
- Reconciling-item categories: new contracts not yet on the schedule, contract modifications, churn or cancellations.
- Aging: deferred balances should decline as revenue is recognized; long-tail deferred balances may indicate stalled performance obligations.
- Special concerns: ASC 606 / IFRS 15 specifics (variable consideration, contract modifications, principal vs. agent), customer churn affecting deferred balance recovery, sales-tax-collected balances separated.

### Pattern R9 — Suspense and clearing accounts

- Source: zero. A suspense or clearing account should net to zero (or to known items) at every close.
- Cadence: monthly, with a daily target of zero where operationally feasible.
- Matching: each entry in must have a matching entry out within a defined window (default 5 business days).
- Reconciling-item categories: any item still open. Suspense items above 30 days are findings.
- Aging: aged suspense is the most common audit finding because suspense accounts are where mistakes go to die.
- Special concerns: suspense should be reviewed weekly, not just at close.

## Outputs

The skill returns two artifacts:

1. `reconciliation_design` — the process spec describing source, cadence, matching rules, reconciling-item handling, exceptions queue, review, escalation.
2. `rec_template` — the workpaper template the owner fills in each cycle.

## Examples

**Input (placeholder):** "Account: prepaid expenses. We have ~30 active prepaid contracts ranging $1k to $400k. Current pain: schedule is in a single spreadsheet that has grown unwieldy, and we found a $40k variance last month we couldn't explain. Cadence: monthly."

**Agent reasoning (abbreviated):**

- Family: prepaid (Pattern R4).
- Source: amortization schedule (one tab per prepaid item).
- Independence: weak (internal). Compensating control: second-person review of additions and the monthly amortization JE before posting; quarterly spot-check against original invoices.
- Cadence: monthly. Mid-month review of additions added that month so they're cleanly on the schedule before close.
- Matching rules: per-line schedule balance roll-forward; total schedule balance equals prepaid GL balance.
- Reconciling-item categories likely sources of the $40k variance: amortization not posted for a contract that ended, a new prepaid added without the corresponding schedule line, a duplicate amortization run.
- Aging rule: any line not moving month-over-month is flagged.
- Workpaper redesign: one summary tab with the rec, one tab per prepaid item with its roll-forward, an exceptions tab that captures any item in dispute.
- Escalation: the $40k variance is age zero at the moment of finding; it must be resolved within the current close window. If unresolved, post to suspense with a memo and resolve within 30 days.

**Output (abbreviated):**

```
## Prepaid Expenses Reconciliation Design

Family: Prepaid (R4)
Cadence: monthly
Owner: GL accountant (preparer); senior accountant (reviewer); controller (final sign-off for items > $25k addition)

### Source
Amortization schedule maintained in the accounting team's shared spreadsheet, one tab per prepaid contract.

### Matching rules
- Sum of remaining balances across all tabs equals the Prepaid Expense GL account.
- The monthly amortization JE total equals the sum of the monthly amortization column across tabs.
- Each new prepaid added in the period has a corresponding invoice in AP.

### Reconciling items
Categorized as: new prepaid (this period), early termination, schedule error.
Aging buckets: 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+.

### Variance investigation protocol
For variance > $5k: investigate per tab; identify which contract's roll-forward does not check.

...
```

## Limitations

- The skill produces a design; it does not perform a rec for a specific period. Execution lives in whatever spreadsheet, rec automation tool, or in-ERP module the company uses.
- The skill recommends matching tools by transaction volume but does not select or configure specific software vendors. The choice between manual matching, semi-automated matching, and a true matching engine depends on company-specific tooling that the skill does not know.
- For accounts whose "independent" source is itself an internal calculation (notably accrued liabilities), the skill notes the weak independence and recommends compensating controls, but it cannot manufacture independence that does not exist.
- The skill assumes the GL is the system of record. For companies in transition between ERPs, or with significant off-ERP bookkeeping, the rec design will need adjustment to handle the data sources actually available.
- Aged reconciling items are flagged and an aging policy is proposed; the skill does not negotiate write-off thresholds with the auditor or with the company's policy committee.
- Multi-currency accounts have rec implications (FX revaluation reconciliations specifically) that the skill addresses at a high level; complex multi-currency consolidation rec needs domain specialization.
- Industry-specific accounts (insurance reserves, loan loss reserves, hedge accounting collateral) require specialist reconciliation patterns beyond the general pattern library here.
- The skill does not perform substantive procedures over the source itself. If the bank statement is fraudulent or the sub-ledger is corrupted, the rec will reconcile to a wrong source successfully; detecting that requires controls beyond reconciliation (bank confirmations, IT general controls).
