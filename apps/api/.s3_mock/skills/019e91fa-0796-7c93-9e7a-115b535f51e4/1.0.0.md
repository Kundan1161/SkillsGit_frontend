# Statistical Analysis Plan Writer

## When to use

**This skill produces methodology guidance, not regulatory advice or final clinical-trial documents. Every output must be reviewed by qualified clinical-research staff, biostatisticians, and regulatory counsel. No skill output may be submitted to a regulator or used to enroll a patient without sponsor sign-off.**

A statistical analysis plan (SAP) is the pre-specified, signed-and-locked document that defines exactly how the trial's data will be analyzed *before* unblinded data are seen. The protocol describes the trial; the SAP describes the analytic operations. The discipline of pre-specification is what allows the trial's primary inferential claim to survive regulatory scrutiny — every analytic decision made after seeing the data is a credibility loss, and most controversial trial outcomes are downstream of an SAP that was too vague or too late.

Use this skill when a sponsor or contract research organization has a protocol that has reached late-draft or final stage and now needs the SAP that operationalizes it. Typical entry points: the lead biostatistician building the SAP from scratch and wanting an opinionated scaffold; an academic trial team being asked by an ethics committee or funder to formalize their analysis plan; a regulatory affairs reviewer checking that the SAP covers the items ICH E9 and E9(R1) expect; a CRO biostatistician onboarding to a sponsor-defined protocol and needing the SAP completed against the sponsor's templates.

Do **not** use this skill to produce the SAP that will go to a regulator. The output is a methodology-aligned scaffold and recommendation set. A qualified biostatistician must finalize, lock, and sign the SAP, and a sponsor's medical, regulatory, and quality teams must review it before locking. The SAP is also the source of the table-figure-listing (TFL) shells that drive programming; the skill flags this dependency but does not produce final TFLs.

Out of scope: Bayesian SAPs with bespoke priors and decision rules (the skill can produce a scaffold but specialist Bayesian biostatistician input is essential); SAPs for master-protocol designs (basket / umbrella / platform); SAPs for diagnostic-accuracy studies (different analytic structure); SAPs for bioequivalence (different regulatory expectations); SAPs for real-world-evidence (RWE) studies (often need target-trial-emulation framing distinct from this skill); SAPs for retrospective observational analyses (cohort, case-control, registry — different bias structure).

## How to apply

Treat SAP authoring as a contract between the sponsor and the inferential claim. Every analytic decision must be pre-specified with rationale. Where the protocol is silent, the SAP completes it; where the protocol is ambiguous, the SAP disambiguates with an audit trail. The discipline mechanism is the estimand framework under ICH E9(R1): every endpoint of inferential interest receives the same five-attribute treatment (population, treatment condition, variable, intercurrent-event strategy, population-level summary). Without estimands, missing-data and intercurrent-event handling becomes ad-hoc; with estimands, the analytic choices follow.

1. **Anchor on the estimand for the primary endpoint.** State the five attributes precisely. **Population** — randomized adults meeting eligibility, with any further restrictions defined. **Treatment condition of interest** — the treatments as randomized, with the comparison being the difference (or ratio, or hazard ratio) between them. **Variable** — the endpoint measurement including timepoint, instrument, and aggregation rule (single timepoint, change from baseline, AUC, time-to-event, responder). **Intercurrent-event strategies** — per intercurrent event (treatment discontinuation, rescue medication, study withdrawal, terminal event such as death when the endpoint is not mortality, surgery, hospitalization), one of: treatment-policy (observe and use post-event data as-is), hypothetical (analyze as if event had not occurred), composite (event becomes part of the endpoint, e.g., non-response if discontinuation), while-on-treatment (observation while on treatment only), principal-stratum (analyze in a subgroup defined by potential-outcomes counterfactuals). Each intercurrent event needs an explicit strategy. **Population-level summary** — the inferential quantity (mean difference, risk difference, odds ratio, hazard ratio, restricted mean survival time difference). The estimand makes the primary analysis nearly mechanical.

2. **Define the analysis populations.** **Intention-to-treat (ITT)** — all randomized participants, analyzed in the arm to which they were randomized. **Modified ITT (mITT)** — sometimes defined as all randomized participants who received at least one dose; the deviation from strict ITT must be justified. **Per-protocol (PP)** — randomized participants who substantially complied with the protocol; specify the compliance threshold. **Safety** — all randomized participants who received at least one dose, analyzed by actual treatment received. **Evaluable** — variations depending on the endpoint (PK evaluable, immunogenicity evaluable, biomarker evaluable). For superiority trials, ITT is the primary; PP is supportive. For non-inferiority trials, both ITT and PP are co-primary or jointly required, depending on the regulator. Document the population per analysis.

3. **Specify the primary analysis method.** The method should be the most efficient unbiased test consistent with the estimand. For continuous endpoints with longitudinal measurement, **mixed-model-for-repeated-measures (MMRM)** with an unstructured covariance, treatment-by-time interaction, baseline-by-time interaction, and a treatment-policy or hypothetical estimand alignment is the contemporary default. For binary endpoints, logistic regression with covariate adjustment for stratification factors and key baseline covariates is the contemporary default; the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test is acceptable for simpler designs. For time-to-event endpoints, the Cox proportional-hazards model stratified by randomization-strata is the default, with a log-rank test as a robust alternative when proportional hazards is questionable; restricted mean survival time (RMST) is increasingly preferred when non-proportional hazards are anticipated. For count endpoints, negative-binomial regression with offset for exposure time. For ordinal endpoints, proportional-odds logistic regression with the proportional-odds assumption tested. Name the model, the covariates, and the convergence-handling rules.

4. **Pre-specify the missing-data handling.** Missing-data methods follow from the estimand. Under a treatment-policy estimand, observed post-discontinuation data are used, and missing data are handled by the primary model's mechanism (MMRM uses likelihood-based inference under missing-at-random within the model). Under a hypothetical estimand for treatment discontinuation, post-discontinuation data are set to missing and imputed under a plausible counterfactual mechanism — typically multiple imputation (MI) with a control-based pattern (jump-to-reference, copy-reference, or washout). The SAP must state: the assumed missing-data mechanism (MAR, MNAR with a specified pattern, MCAR); the primary imputation method; the imputation model covariates; the number of imputations; the combining rules (Rubin's rules). State the rationale.

5. **Pre-specify the multiplicity-control strategy.** If there is a single primary endpoint, no multiplicity correction is required for primary. If there are multiple primary endpoints, multiple co-primary comparisons, or a family of key secondary endpoints that will be tested with type-I error control, specify the procedure. **Hierarchical (fixed-sequence) gatekeeping** is appropriate when the family has a clear clinical ordering and the test at each step uses the full alpha conditional on prior-step significance. **Graphical procedures** (Bretz, Maurer, Brannath, Posch — graphical multiple-testing procedure) generalize hierarchical and Bonferroni-Holm structures by allowing alpha to recycle between endpoints; appropriate when the family is complex and partial-significance scenarios matter. **Hochberg, Holm, or Hommel** are step-up or step-down procedures with broad family-wise-error control. **Dunnett** is the standard for multi-arm trials comparing several actives to one control. State which procedure controls the family-wise error rate, list the endpoints in the family, and define the alpha allocation graph if a graphical procedure is used.

6. **Pre-specify the secondary and exploratory analyses.** Each key secondary endpoint receives its own estimand attributes, analysis population, analysis method, and place in the multiplicity hierarchy. Other secondary endpoints are typically analyzed under the same population and method conventions but without multiplicity protection; state explicitly that they are not inferentially controlled. Exploratory endpoints are explicitly labeled as hypothesis-generating with no inferential claim.

7. **Pre-specify the sensitivity analyses.** A sensitivity analysis stresses one assumption of the primary analysis to test the robustness of the conclusion. Pre-specify: a tipping-point analysis on the missing-data mechanism (varying the imputation departure from MAR until the conclusion flips); an alternative estimand analysis (e.g., treatment-policy primary, hypothetical sensitivity); an alternative model specification (e.g., MMRM with unstructured covariance primary, with autoregressive covariance sensitivity); a per-protocol-population sensitivity for non-inferiority and equivalence trials; a covariate-adjusted versus unadjusted sensitivity; for time-to-event, a proportional-hazards diagnostic and an RMST or stratified-test sensitivity. Sensitivity analyses are not multiplicity-adjusted; their purpose is robustness, not additional inferential claims.

8. **Pre-specify subgroup analyses.** Subgroups identified in the protocol (age, sex, region, baseline disease severity, prior-therapy exposure, biomarker positivity) get pre-specified subgroup analyses. State whether subgroup analyses are inferentially powered (rare in confirmatory trials), descriptive (most common), or part of a pre-specified subgroup-of-interest gating in adaptive designs. Use forest plots for visualization; interpret with the appropriate caution about subgroup interactions. Avoid post-hoc subgroup definition.

9. **Pre-specify the interim-analysis structure.** For group-sequential designs, define: the number of interim analyses, the information-fraction timing (the actual interim sample size or event count required to trigger), the alpha-spending function (O'Brien-Fleming, Pocock, Hwang-Shih-DeCani with γ), the beta-spending function for futility (if applicable), the boundary type (efficacy-only, futility-only, both), the boundary computation method (computed using qualified software at the time of each look), the data-monitoring-committee (DMC) charter cross-reference, the firewall between blinded and unblinded data (only unblinded statisticians and the DMC see unblinded interim results), the conditional-power computation if applicable, and the rules for early stopping (binding versus non-binding). State that the boundaries will be re-computed based on the actual information fraction at the time of the look.

10. **Pre-specify the safety analysis.** Adverse events are summarized descriptively by treatment group, with no inferential claim unless a specific safety hypothesis is pre-specified. Summaries by SOC and PT (MedDRA-coded), by severity, by relationship-to-study-drug, by seriousness, and by leading-to-discontinuation. Laboratory values summarized by mean change from baseline and by shift-table (e.g., normal-to-abnormal). Vital signs and ECG similarly. Concomitant medications coded with WHODrug and summarized. State the MedDRA and WHODrug versions to be used (the version is locked at the time of database lock).

11. **Pre-specify the dataset-creation conventions.** State whether the analysis datasets will be created in CDISC ADaM format (mandatory for FDA / PMDA submissions, expected by EMA, increasingly expected globally), with traceability to SDTM. List the ADaM datasets to be created (ADSL, ADAE, ADLB, ADVS, ADTTE, ADEFF or ADXX for the primary-endpoint dataset, ADPP for per-protocol flagging, etc.). Reference the sponsor's standard analysis-dataset conventions if any. Document the derived variables that the SAP relies on (analysis populations, treatment-emergent AE flags, change-from-baseline derivations).

12. **Pre-specify the display shells.** The SAP typically includes either inline or appendixed table-figure-listing (TFL) shells. Each shell defines the population, the layout, the variables, and the statistical content. Shells become the input to programming and the basis for the unblinded results. Locking the shells in the SAP discipline ensures the team is not making display-level decisions after seeing data.

13. **Pre-specify the timing of SAP locking.** The SAP must be signed and locked before database lock and unblinding for the primary analysis. For interim analyses, the SAP must be signed and locked before the data cut for the interim. State the version-control approach (numbered versions, signature dates) and the amendment process (any post-lock change is an amendment with documented rationale and a version-bump; post-unblinding changes are post-hoc and lose pre-specification credit).

14. **Cross-check internal consistency.** Before returning, verify: every endpoint listed in the protocol has an estimand and an analysis method in the SAP; every analysis population referenced in the analysis methods is defined; multiplicity-control covers the inferentially-claimed endpoints; missing-data methods are consistent with the estimand intercurrent-event strategies; sensitivity analyses cover the main assumptions of the primary analysis; the interim-analysis structure is consistent with the protocol's design section; the safety section is non-trivial; the dataset-creation conventions and TFL shells exist.

15. **Self-check before returning.** Confirm: estimand framework is present and complete for the primary endpoint; primary analysis method is named, with model and covariates specified; missing-data method is named and tied to the estimand; multiplicity strategy is explicit; sensitivity analyses are pre-specified; interim-analysis structure (if applicable) is complete and aligned with the DMC charter; the SAP-locking-before-unblinding requirement is stated; the recommendation that a qualified biostatistician must lock and sign is explicit.

## Examples

### Example 1 — Two-arm Phase 3 RCT, continuous primary endpoint with MMRM

**Inputs:**
- `protocol_summary`: Phase 3 RCT, double-blind, parallel-group, [IP] vs placebo, 24-week treatment period.
- `primary_endpoint`: change from baseline in [score] at Week 24, measured at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24.
- `secondary_endpoints`: `[{"name":"responder_24w","def":"≥50% improvement at Week 24"},{"name":"qol_24w","def":"change in [QoL instrument] total score"},{"name":"time_to_response","def":"time to first ≥30% improvement"}]`
- `design_type`: superiority_two_arm_parallel
- `multiplicity_approach`: hierarchical_gatekeeping

**Output sap_draft (excerpt):**

> **Estimand for the primary endpoint.** Population: randomized adults meeting eligibility. Treatment condition of interest: [IP] versus placebo, administered as randomized over 24 weeks. Variable: change from baseline in [score] at Week 24. Intercurrent events: (a) treatment discontinuation — treatment-policy (observed post-discontinuation data used) as primary, with hypothetical (post-discontinuation data set missing and multiply imputed under a control-based jump-to-reference pattern) as sensitivity; (b) rescue medication use — hypothetical (analyze as if no rescue had occurred); (c) death (rare; if observed, composite as worst-rank within the model). Population-level summary: difference in least-squares mean change from baseline at Week 24, [IP] minus placebo, with 95% confidence interval.
>
> **Primary analysis method.** MMRM on the change-from-baseline outcome at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, with fixed effects for treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline score, baseline-by-visit interaction, and stratification factors; unstructured covariance; restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation; Kenward-Roger denominator degrees-of-freedom. Primary inference is the Week-24 contrast of treatment versus placebo.
>
> **Multiplicity control.** Hierarchical gatekeeping at alpha 0.05 two-sided in the order: (1) primary endpoint; (2) responder at Week 24; (3) QoL change at Week 24; (4) time-to-response. Each step is tested at full alpha conditional on all prior steps being significant. If any step fails, subsequent steps are reported descriptively without inferential claim.
>
> **Missing data.** Primary MMRM uses likelihood-based inference under missing-at-random within the model. Sensitivity analysis: multiple imputation under jump-to-reference (post-discontinuation data set missing and imputed using the placebo arm's post-baseline distribution) and copy-reference patterns; 100 imputations; Rubin's combining rules. Tipping-point analysis: progressively shift the imputed values in the [IP] arm toward worse outcomes by increments of 0.1 standard deviations until the primary contrast loses significance; report the shift required.
>
> **Sensitivity analyses.** (1) Per-protocol population. (2) Treatment-policy primary versus hypothetical sensitivity (already specified). (3) MMRM with autoregressive covariance versus unstructured covariance. (4) Adjusted versus unadjusted for stratification factors. (5) Worst-case last-observation-carried-forward (LOCF) — historically informative but not regulatorily preferred. (6) Tipping-point for missing-data assumption.
>
> **Analysis populations.** ITT — all randomized; PP — randomized and substantially compliant (compliance ≥ 80% of expected doses, completion of Week 24 visit within 7 days of target, no major protocol deviations as defined in Appendix B); Safety — randomized and dosed; Evaluable — analysis-specific (PK evaluable, etc.).

**Excerpt of estimand_table:**

| Endpoint | Population | Treatment condition | Variable | Intercurrent-event strategy | Population summary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Randomized adults meeting eligibility | [IP] vs placebo as randomized, 24 weeks | Change in [score], baseline to Week 24 | Discontinuation: treatment-policy primary; rescue: hypothetical | LSMD at Week 24, 95% CI |
| Responder Wk24 | Same | Same | ≥50% improvement at Week 24 | Discontinuation: composite (non-responder); rescue: hypothetical | Risk difference, 95% CI |
| QoL Wk24 | Same | Same | Change in [QoL], baseline to Week 24 | Same | LSMD, 95% CI |
| Time to response | Same | Same | Time to first ≥30% improvement | Discontinuation: censored at discontinuation; rescue: censored | Hazard ratio, 95% CI |

### Example 2 — Non-inferiority trial, time-to-event endpoint

**Inputs:**
- `design_type`: non_inferiority
- `primary_endpoint`: time to disease progression or death from any cause (PFS) at the planned analysis information fraction
- `multiplicity_approach`: none_single_primary
- `interim_analyses`: `{"count":1,"info_fraction":0.7,"alpha_spending":"OBrien-Fleming","futility":"non-binding_HSD_gamma_-2"}`

**Output sap_draft (excerpt):**

> **Estimand for the primary endpoint.** Population: randomized adults with [disease] meeting eligibility. Treatment condition of interest: [IP] versus active comparator as randomized. Variable: time from randomization to disease progression (per RECIST v[X] independent central review) or death from any cause. Intercurrent events: new anticancer therapy prior to progression — censored at last evaluable assessment before new therapy (treatment-policy variant with sensitivity using treatment-policy without censoring); withdrawal — censored at last evaluable assessment; loss to follow-up — censored at last evaluable assessment. Population-level summary: hazard ratio [IP] versus comparator with 95% confidence interval; non-inferiority claim if the upper bound of the 95% CI excludes the pre-specified margin of [Δ].
>
> **Primary analysis.** Stratified Cox proportional-hazards model on the ITT population, stratified by randomization-strata; PP population co-primary. Proportional-hazards assumption assessed via Schoenfeld residuals and a treatment-by-log-time interaction test; if violated, restricted mean survival time (RMST) at the truncation time of [τ] reported as a robust sensitivity.
>
> **Interim analysis.** One interim at 70% information using O'Brien-Fleming alpha-spending; one-sided alpha 0.025 across the trial. Non-binding futility using HSD γ=−2 spending. The DMC charter specifies the unblinded statistician access, the DMC composition, the meeting cadence, and the stopping recommendations the DMC may make. The sponsor remains blinded.
>
> **Multiplicity.** Single primary endpoint; no multiplicity adjustment for the primary inference. Secondary endpoints reported descriptively with 95% CIs and no inferential claim of non-inferiority or superiority.

## Limitations

- **Not a finished SAP.** Output is a structured draft requiring qualified-biostatistician finalization, sponsor sign-off, and lock-before-unblinding discipline.
- **Estimand drafting requires sponsor input.** The skill drafts a recommended estimand under reasonable defaults, but the sponsor's clinical and regulatory team owns the final estimand. Intercurrent-event strategy choices have substantive clinical and regulatory implications.
- **Multiplicity is a deep area.** Graphical procedures and gatekeeping structures have nuances (alpha recycling, partial-significance handling, transitions through fail-pass states) that the skill scaffolds but does not exhaustively cover.
- **Missing-data assumptions are unverifiable.** Even pre-specified methods rest on assumptions about the missing-data mechanism that cannot be verified from the observed data; tipping-point and pattern-mixture sensitivities are partial mitigations, not proofs.
- **No simulation operating characteristics.** For confirmatory adaptive designs, the SAP's operating characteristics (type I error under the null, power under alternatives, expected sample size) must be confirmed via simulation in qualified software. The skill does not simulate.
- **CDISC ADaM specifics** vary by therapeutic area and by sponsor convention. The skill names the ADaM datasets at a generic level; the detailed metadata-driven specifications are a separate document.
- **Bayesian SAPs** with priors, decision rules, and predictive-probability stopping require specialist input that the skill flags but does not provide.
- **Regulatory negotiation.** SAPs are often discussed with regulators in advance for confirmatory studies (FDA Type C / pre-NDA meetings, EMA scientific advice). The skill produces a defensible draft; the regulatory affairs team owns negotiation.
