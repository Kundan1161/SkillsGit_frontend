# AI Image Prompt Designer

## When to use

Use this skill when a contributor needs a written, reproducible plan for prompting a diffusion image model and iterating toward a target. The plan covers the structure of the positive prompt (subject, action, environment, lighting, medium, composition, technical descriptors), the structure of the negative prompt (what the generator should *avoid* drawing), the sampler and classifier-free-guidance choice, the step count, the aspect ratio and resolution, the seed-control strategy that lets the contributor reproduce and explore variants, and the iteration ladder that walks from a rough draft to a settled image without thrashing.

The skill is downstream of any creative brief; it sits alongside `controlnet-conditioning-architect` (which locks composition through spatial conditioning) and `lora-and-finetune-methodology` (which trains style or subject adapters). The output is text — a prompt plan — and not an image. The skill is appropriate for SD 1.5, SDXL, SD 3.x, Flux-class transformers, and other text-to-image diffusion or rectified-flow models; the structural advice is shared, with model-specific CFG and sampler defaults called out.

**Mandatory safety disclaimer.** This skill produces methodology guidance for diffusion-based image generation. The skill does not address the copyright status of training data or generated outputs in any jurisdiction; consult counsel for commercial use. The skill does not produce or recommend NSFW, defamatory, infringing, or person-impersonating content; usage must respect platform policy and applicable law.

## How to apply

The skill walks a ten-stage pipeline. Early stages set the brief and choose the model family. Middle stages compose the positive and negative prompts, choose the sampler and CFG, decide the aspect and seed strategy. Late stages plan the iteration ladder and emit the deliverable.

### Stage 1 — Restate the brief in operational language

1. From `brief` derive a one-sentence target: subject, action, environment, mood, medium. "A cinematic portrait of a woman in a rain-soaked alley, mid-thirties, contemplative, neon-lit, photographic style" is enough to anchor the prompt; "make something cool" is not.
2. Capture the *consumer* of the image. A marketing thumbnail tolerates a different failure profile than a hero shot on a landing page, a printed asset, or a reference plate that will be composited downstream. Higher-stakes consumers justify tighter iteration and more deliberate sampler choice.
3. Identify the *one anchor* the image must keep. The anchor is the single attribute the contributor will refuse to compromise — subject identity, exact composition, brand colour, specific lighting. Everything else is negotiable. The anchor drives the negative prompt and the iteration ladder.
4. Capture explicit *exclusions*. "No people," "no text," "no watermarks," "no centred composition" are anti-goals; they belong in the negative prompt and in the prompt-design notes.

### Stage 2 — Choose the model family and its defaults

5. Identify the model family from `model_and_constraints` or infer from the platform. SD 1.5 tolerates dense token prompts, prefers CFG 7-9, runs well at 512 short-edge with upscale. SDXL prefers shorter prompts, CFG 5-7, native 1024 short-edge. SD 3.x and Flux-class rectified-flow models prefer natural-language prompts, lower CFG (3-5) and respond well to longer, descriptive sentences rather than tag soup.
6. Record the *default sampler-and-step pair* for the family. DPM++ 2M Karras at 25-30 steps is a robust default for SD 1.5 and SDXL. Euler / Euler-ancestral with sigma-uniform scheduling is a robust default for SD 3.x and Flux-class. The plan records the choice and the rationale, not just the value.
7. Record the *prompt grammar* the family expects. SD 1.5 and SDXL respond to comma-delimited descriptive tokens with explicit weights (e.g. `(keyword:1.2)`). SD 3.x and Flux-class respond to descriptive prose; weighting syntax has diminished effect, and the model parses paragraphs.
8. Record the *negative-prompt support*. SD 1.5 and SDXL have first-class negative-prompt support and almost always benefit from one. Flux-class and some SD 3.x configurations either lack negative-prompt support or require a `true_cfg`-style two-pass schedule; in those cases the plan moves exclusions into the positive prompt as descriptive avoidance or relies on classifier-free auxiliary tools.

### Stage 3 — Compose the positive prompt

9. Build the positive prompt as an ordered stack of six bands: subject, action and pose, environment and background, lighting, medium and style, composition and technical descriptors. Order matters — most diffusion models attend more strongly to early tokens. The anchor from Stage 1 sits in the subject band.
10. The *subject band* names what is in the frame and its essential attributes (age range, build, clothing, distinguishing features). It is the most concrete band and should not contain abstract mood words.
11. The *action/pose band* names what the subject is doing and how. "Standing, leaning against a railing, looking off-camera" is operational; "feeling melancholy" is not, and belongs in the lighting/mood band.
12. The *environment band* names where the scene is, the time of day, weather, and significant props. Environment grounds the model's spatial expectations and reduces drift.
13. The *lighting band* names the lighting source, direction, intensity, colour temperature, and mood. "Backlit by sodium street lamps, hard shadows, warm key from a window" gives the model handles for both colour and form.
14. The *medium/style band* names the medium (photograph, oil painting, 3D render, vector illustration, charcoal sketch) and the style. This skill avoids naming living artists or copyrighted franchises; it uses medium descriptors and movement names (Bauhaus, Art Nouveau, Dutch tonalism, neo-noir) and concrete craft descriptors (35mm film grain, Kodak Portra colour palette, hand-painted matte). The plan calls out that style-by-artist-name is both a copyright and a brand-fidelity risk and recommends LoRAs trained on licensed corpora when a specific look is required.
15. The *composition/technical band* names framing (close-up, medium shot, wide), camera angle (eye level, low angle, overhead), focal length, depth of field, aspect ratio, and any technical anchors (rule of thirds, leading lines, negative space).
16. Prune ruthlessly. Each token competes for attention; redundant words dilute the bands the contributor cares about. The plan recommends fewer than 75 tokens for SD 1.5/SDXL and natural-language paragraphs of 40-80 words for SD 3.x and Flux-class.

### Stage 4 — Compose the negative prompt

17. The negative prompt removes failure modes from the search space. The plan composes the negative prompt as three groups: anatomy and structural defects, aesthetic defects, content exclusions.
18. *Anatomy and structural defects.* "Extra fingers, fused fingers, malformed hands, extra limbs, missing limbs, distorted face, asymmetric eyes, mangled teeth, broken anatomy, deformed." For SDXL with newer base checkpoints, this group is often shorter than for SD 1.5.
19. *Aesthetic defects.* "Plastic skin, waxy skin, oversaturated, blown highlights, oversharpened, JPEG artefacts, watermark, signature, logo, low quality, blurry, out of focus, text, lettering."
20. *Content exclusions.* Whatever the brief specifies as anti-goals — "no people in background, no cars, no centred composition, no text on signs" — phrased in concrete vocabulary the model understands.
21. Avoid stacking the negative prompt with tokens the positive prompt did not invite. A negative prompt of "horror, gore, violence, ugly" when the positive prompt did not lean horror simply burns guidance budget; the model never proposed those tokens in the first place.
22. For models without classical negative-prompt support, the plan rewrites exclusions as positive descriptions of avoidance ("clean photographic skin, natural eyes, well-formed hands") and notes that the effect is weaker.

### Stage 5 — Choose sampler, steps, CFG, and scheduler

23. The plan records sampler, step count, CFG, and scheduler with a one-line rationale.
24. *Sampler defaults.* DPM++ 2M with Karras schedule for SDXL and SD 1.5 at 25-30 steps is the robust workhorse. DPM++ SDE Karras gives softer, more painterly outputs at 20-25 steps but is non-deterministic across some backends; record the determinism caveat. UniPC at 15-20 steps is a fast option for batches. Euler / Euler-ancestral at 20-30 steps with sigma-uniform or simple scheduler is the default for SD 3.x and Flux-class.
25. *Step count.* More steps does not mean better images past a model-specific plateau. 25-30 is the workhorse band; sweep 15, 25, 40 only when diagnosing whether a defect is convergence-related rather than prompt-related.
26. *CFG.* SD 1.5 likes 7-9. SDXL likes 5-7. SD 3.x and Flux-class like 3-5. Above the family's ceiling, prompt adherence increases but artefacts (waxy skin, doubled features, colour banding, "fried" look) appear; below the floor, the image drifts from prompt. The plan records a default and a sweep range (default minus one, default, default plus two) for the iteration ladder.
27. *Scheduler.* Karras schedules for DPM++ samplers; sigma-uniform for SD 3.x and Flux-class samplers; SGM-uniform for many Flux workflows. Mixing the wrong scheduler with the wrong sampler is a common silent defect that produces washed or mushy outputs.
28. Document which choices are *deterministic* under the backend in use. Some sampler-and-scheduler pairs are deterministic across runs (same seed -> same pixels), others have intentional stochasticity ("ancestral" samplers). The seed strategy depends on this.

### Stage 6 — Aspect ratio and resolution

29. Choose the aspect ratio from the consumer (web hero is 16:9 or 21:9; portrait is 2:3 or 3:4; square is 1:1; story format is 9:16). Train-time bucket alignment matters: SDXL was trained on aspect buckets near 1:1, 2:3, 3:4, 16:9 at 1024 short edge; deviating significantly from a trained bucket increases composition defects.
30. Choose the *generation resolution* and *final resolution* separately. Generation resolution is what the diffusion run produces; final resolution is what the upscale chain produces. Generating significantly above the model's native bucket invites duplication artefacts (two heads, two centres); generating below invites detail loss.
31. The plan records the upscale path — latent upscale and a second pass at lower denoise (0.3-0.5), or tile-based upscaler (Ultimate SD Upscale, tiled diffusion), or external upscaler (ESRGAN family). For face-heavy images the plan calls out a face-restoration pass and the order in which it runs (after upscale, before colour grade).
32. Composition descriptors in the positive prompt should agree with the aspect ratio. "Wide cinematic frame" with a 1:1 generation invites a centred subject and wasted vertical space; "vertical portrait" with a 16:9 generation invites mirrored or duplicated subjects.

### Stage 7 — Seed strategy

33. Decide whether the run is *exploratory* (broad seed sweep to find a candidate) or *deterministic* (lock to a seed to vary one parameter at a time). Both are legitimate; mixing them silently is a common cause of "I cannot tell what changed."
34. Exploratory phase: generate a batch of 8-16 seeds at the default settings to surface candidates. Tag the seeds by composition family (front-facing centred, three-quarter, profile, environmental wide) so a candidate can be picked for refinement.
35. Deterministic phase: lock the seed for the chosen candidate. Vary one parameter at a time — CFG, sampler, one prompt band — and compare. Variance across multiple knobs at once produces unattributable results.
36. Record seeds in the plan. Reproducibility of a settled image requires the seed, the prompt, the negative prompt, the model checkpoint hash, the sampler, the scheduler, the step count, the CFG, the resolution, and the precision (fp16/bf16/fp8). Missing any one of these can change pixels.
37. For Flux-class and some SD 3.x configurations, even with a fixed seed, results may not be bit-exact across different backends or precision modes. The plan calls out backend-pinning when bit-exact reproducibility is required.

### Stage 8 — Iteration ladder

38. Compose an iteration ladder — a sequence of ordered changes to try, each isolating one variable. The ladder is short (five to seven steps), targeted, and oriented to the failure modes in `prior_attempts`.
39. *Step 1 — anchor lock.* Confirm the anchor is present in 4-of-8 seeds at default settings. If not, the prompt's subject band needs strengthening, not the CFG.
40. *Step 2 — composition fix.* If anchor is present but composition is wrong, adjust the composition band (framing, angle, focal length) and re-batch on the same seed family.
41. *Step 3 — lighting fix.* If composition is right but mood is wrong, adjust the lighting band; do not chase mood through medium-style changes.
42. *Step 4 — anatomy and defect cleanup.* If structure is right but defects persist, extend the negative prompt for the observed defect family or call out the face-restoration / hand-restoration tool path in the downstream pipeline.
43. *Step 5 — CFG sweep.* Lock seed and prompt; vary CFG across (default-1, default, default+1, default+2). Note where artefacts begin.
44. *Step 6 — sampler swap.* Lock seed, prompt, and CFG; try one alternative sampler/scheduler pair. Many "I cannot get this look" sessions resolve here rather than in more prompt tokens.
45. *Step 7 — escalate.* If the ladder ends without convergence, the issue is structural — composition needs ControlNet conditioning, style needs a LoRA, identity needs IP-Adapter or fine-tune. The plan hands off to those skills rather than continuing to torture the prompt.

### Stage 9 — Risk and ethics notes

46. Style-by-artist-name. The plan does not endorse style transfer that names living artists or copyrighted properties. Recommend mood, movement, and craft descriptors instead, and recommend a licensed LoRA when a specific look is mandatory.
47. Person impersonation. The plan refuses to generate prompts that target a recognisable real person without explicit consent and platform allowance. Public-figure synthesis carries defamation, right-of-publicity, and platform-policy risk and is out of scope here.
48. NSFW. The plan does not produce NSFW prompts; downstream platform policy may forbid them regardless.
49. Training-data provenance. The diffusion model's training corpus is the model author's responsibility; outputs may resemble copyrighted inputs in unpredictable ways. Commercial use is a counsel question, not a prompt-engineering question.
50. Watermark and provenance. Where supported, recommend adding C2PA or platform watermark metadata to generated assets so the asset chain is auditable downstream.

### Stage 10 — Compose the deliverable

51. Open with a one-paragraph *prompt intent* statement: what the prompt is producing, for whom, on what model, against what anchor.
52. Render the plan as a markdown document covering the positive prompt (with bands annotated), the negative prompt (with groups annotated), sampler/CFG/steps/scheduler choice with rationale, aspect/resolution decision with rationale, seed strategy, iteration ladder, downstream upscale and restoration path, and the risk notes.
53. Emit `prompt_json` with fields: `positive` (string), `negative` (string), `sampler`, `scheduler`, `steps`, `cfg`, `aspect_ratio`, `generation_resolution`, `final_resolution`, `seeds` (exploration list + locked seed slot), `iteration_ladder` (ordered list of steps), `risk_notes`.
54. Close with the mandatory safety disclaimer and a "what this skill does not do" note pointing the contributor at `controlnet-conditioning-architect`, `lora-and-finetune-methodology`, and `production-batch-rendering-pipeline` as appropriate.

## Examples

**Input (placeholder):**

`brief`: "Cinematic still — a tea house at dusk in a rainy old town, single lantern lit, an empty wooden bench in the foreground; mood is contemplative, quiet. Will be a desktop wallpaper, 21:9."

`model_and_constraints`: "SDXL base + a community photographic checkpoint; one 16GB GPU; budget is twenty minutes."

`aspect_and_resolution`: "21:9 ultra-wide, final 3840x1640 after upscale."

`prior_attempts`: "Earlier attempts produced two lanterns, distorted bench, blown highlights on the lantern."

**Plan (abbreviated):**

- Intent: a quiet, contemplative dusk scene, single warm lantern as the anchor, environmental wide framing, photographic medium.
- Model family: SDXL; sampler DPM++ 2M Karras; steps 28; CFG 6; scheduler Karras; generation 1536x640 (close to a trained ultra-wide bucket).
- Positive prompt bands: (subject) a small traditional wooden tea house; (action) light rain falling, steam curling from a kettle by the window; (environment) old town stone alley, wet cobblestones, dusk sky, no people; (lighting) single warm lantern as primary key, deep ambient blue from sky, soft reflections on wet stone, low ambient; (medium/style) cinematic photograph, 35mm film grain, Kodak-style colour palette, restrained contrast; (composition) ultra-wide 21:9 framing, low-angle eye level, lantern centred-right at the rule-of-thirds vertical, empty bench in foreground, deep negative space sky upper left.
- Negative prompt: extra lanterns, multiple lanterns, duplicated objects, blown highlights, oversaturated, neon, people, signage, text, watermark, low quality, blurry, oversharpened, plastic surfaces.
- Seed strategy: batch 8 seeds for candidate discovery; lock the best for CFG sweep at 5, 6, 7 and sampler swap to Euler-a.
- Iteration ladder: confirm "single lantern" anchor in 4-of-8 seeds; if duplicated, weight `(single lantern:1.3)` and add "multiple lanterns" to negative; once anchor holds, adjust composition; then lighting; then anatomy of bench; CFG sweep; sampler swap if highlights still blow.
- Upscale: latent upscale 1.5x at denoise 0.35, then ESRGAN-family pass, then mild colour grade; no face restoration needed.
- Risk notes: no real-person reference; medium-style descriptors only (no artist name); platform watermark applied at export.

**Output excerpt:** the markdown plan plus a JSON object whose `positive` is the assembled prompt string, whose `negative` is the assembled negative string, whose `sampler`, `scheduler`, `steps`, `cfg`, `aspect_ratio`, `generation_resolution`, `final_resolution`, `seeds`, `iteration_ladder` are filled with the values above.

## Limitations

- The skill plans prompts; it does not generate images, manage a GPU queue, or verify outputs. A human or downstream automation runs the generation.
- Diffusion model behaviour shifts between checkpoints, LoRAs, and backends. The plan recommends defaults that hold across the common cases; specific community checkpoints may need their own sweep.
- Token attention falls off past roughly 75 tokens in SD 1.5/SDXL and behaves differently in transformer-based diffusion (Flux-class). The plan recommends concision; very long prompts are an antipattern.
- Negative prompts have asymmetric strength across models; the plan flags families where negatives are weak (Flux-class) and recommends positive-form avoidance instead.
- Reproducibility of an image requires pinning the entire stack — checkpoint hash, sampler, scheduler, steps, CFG, seed, resolution, precision, backend version. The plan calls this out; it does not enforce it.
- Style-by-artist-name and person impersonation are out of scope. The plan refuses to construct prompts that target a living artist's style or a recognisable real person.
- The plan addresses *prompt design* not *model alignment*. If a base model is structurally incapable of the target (e.g. lacks the training corpus for the medium requested), no prompt will recover it; the iteration ladder escalates to LoRA or fine-tune at Step 7.
- The skill does not address the copyright status of training data or generated outputs in any jurisdiction; consult counsel for commercial use.
