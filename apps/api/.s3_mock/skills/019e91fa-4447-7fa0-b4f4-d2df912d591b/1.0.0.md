# Medical Evidence Extraction Coordinator

## When to use

Use this skill once the screening phase of a review is producing a stable list of included studies and the team is ready to design the extraction stage. The extraction form is the bridge between the studies and the synthesis. A loose form forces re-extraction; a brittle form forces post-hoc workarounds. The middle ground — comprehensive but field-tested before launch — is the goal.

This skill produces methodology guidance for literature reviews. Outputs are not clinical decisions or care recommendations. Every output must be reviewed by qualified clinical/research staff. Conclusions about treatment efficacy or safety must be confirmed against current clinical practice guidelines and primary literature.

Typical triggers:

- Screening is nearing completion and the synthesis plan is firming up; the team needs a form before extraction begins.
- An ongoing extraction is generating frequent reviewer disagreements and the conflict-resolution rule needs to be revised.
- Many included studies report outcome data in formats incompatible with the planned pooled metric, so imputation rules are needed.
- A pilot showed that the form is missing fields the synthesis will need, and the team must rework before continuing.

Do not use this skill for:

- Authoring the review protocol itself — that is the protocol-author skill's job. This skill operationalizes the extraction sections of an existing protocol.
- Statistical analysis of extracted data — that is the synthesis stage.
- Critical appraisal of individual studies — risk-of-bias work is its own activity, usually done in parallel with extraction by the same reviewers.

## Inputs

- `review_question` (required) — The structured clinical question; drives which variables must be captured.
- `included_designs` — Eligible study designs; determines which design-specific fields are needed (allocation method and blinding for randomized trials, exposure ascertainment and confounder adjustment for observational studies, reference standard for diagnostic studies, follow-up completeness for prognostic studies).
- `planned_synthesis` — Affects field strictness. A quantitative meta-analysis needs precise outcome statistics with documented dispersion; a narrative synthesis can tolerate more text fields.
- `team_constraints` — Number of extractors and statistical capacity. A team of two needs sharper conflict-resolution rules than a team of five. A team without a statistician should avoid imputation rules that need bespoke analysis.
- `existing_form` — If supplied, the skill refines rather than starts from scratch; output keeps original field names where possible.

## How to apply

Apply steps in order. Where information is missing, write `<TODO: ...>` and surface it in `open_questions`. The extraction plan is meant to be a living document during the pilot phase but frozen before independent extraction begins.

### 1. Map variables to synthesis needs

1.1. Start from the **synthesis plan** and work backwards. For each pre-specified analysis, list the inputs required, then build the extraction variables to capture exactly those inputs and nothing speculative.

1.2. Group variables into six standard categories:
   - **Study identification** — first author, year, country, funding source, declared conflicts of interest, study registration identifier, study label used in the review.
   - **Design** — overall design type, randomization method, allocation concealment, blinding (who, how), unit of analysis, follow-up duration, attrition rate.
   - **Population** — sample size at randomization, age (mean, SD or median, IQR), sex, ethnicity if reported, condition severity, key comorbidities, eligibility criteria summary, recruitment setting.
   - **Intervention and comparator** — agent, dose, frequency, route, duration, co-interventions, adherence measure if reported.
   - **Outcomes** — for each pre-specified outcome: definition used by the study, measurement instrument, time point, n at follow-up per arm, summary statistic (mean and SD, count and total, hazard ratio with confidence interval), effect estimate as reported, any subgroup data needed.
   - **Notes** — early stopping, protocol amendments, planned interim analyses, multiplicity adjustment, deviations from registered protocol.

1.3. For every variable, specify: **field name**, **data type** (text, integer, decimal, choice, date, free text), **allowed values** or constraints, **source location** in a typical paper (Methods, Results table, supplement, registration record), and the **extraction rule** that resolves common ambiguities.

1.4. Resist the urge to extract everything. Each extra variable is friction for the extractors and noise in the analysis. If a variable would not be reported, used in a sensitivity analysis, or cited in the discussion, drop it.

### 2. Define outcome-data fields with maximum specificity

2.1. For each outcome named in the protocol, build a sub-form with: the **effect-metric family** the synthesis needs (risk ratio, mean difference, hazard ratio), the **timepoint** the synthesis wants (closest reported timepoint within a tolerance), and the **per-arm statistics** needed.

2.2. Specify **fallback fields** for common reporting variations: count and total when the study reports a percentage and a total; mean and SD when the study reports median and IQR; hazard ratio with CI when only a p-value and event counts are reported. Each fallback has a documented derivation rule.

2.3. Capture **as-reported numbers** in addition to derived numbers. The extracted record should let a future auditor reconstruct the derivation without going back to the paper.

2.4. Pre-specify how **multi-arm trials** are handled — which arms enter the comparison, whether the shared comparator group is split among multiple comparisons, and the convention used in the synthesis software.

2.5. Pre-specify how **cluster-randomized trials** are handled — whether the analysis adjusts for clustering and what the design effect (or intraclass correlation) input is.

2.6. Pre-specify how **crossover trials** are handled — first-period only, paired analysis with within-subject correlation, or exclusion.

### 3. Design the dual-extraction workflow

3.1. The default is **dual independent extraction** for outcome data and design-quality fields, with single-reviewer extraction (and a second-reviewer check) for stable identification fields like authors and year. State the default and any deviations.

3.2. Define the **independent extraction window** — extractors should not see each other's forms until both have submitted. If the extraction tool does not enforce this, use a process control (separate folders, locked rows).

3.3. Set a **calibration set** — the first three to five studies extracted by all reviewers, with a structured meeting to discuss disagreements before independent extraction continues. Record the field-level agreement rate from the calibration to set expectations.

3.4. Plan the **conflict-resolution rule**. Options:
   - Third-reviewer adjudication for fields where exact numerical agreement matters.
   - Consensus discussion for judgment fields (risk-of-bias domains, design classifications).
   - Default-to-paper rule: if extractors disagree on what the paper says, both must show the source location and the most defensible reading wins; if neither is defensible, the field is marked unclear.

3.5. Track **disagreement metrics** by field. A field with persistent disagreement is usually under-specified; revise the extraction rule.

### 4. Plan handling of missing data

4.1. Inventory the categories of missing data the team will encounter:
   - **Missing outcome data** — outcome not reported, reported only as p-value, reported only graphically, reported only for a subgroup.
   - **Missing dispersion measures** — mean reported without SD or SE; count reported without total at the time point.
   - **Missing baseline characteristics** — needed for subgroup analyses or covariate adjustment.
   - **Missing methodological details** — randomization method, allocation concealment, attrition by arm.

4.2. For each category, pre-specify the **handling rule**:
   - First, attempt to derive from other reported quantities (SD from CI, SE from CI, mean from median + IQR + sample size with the appropriate transformation).
   - Second, attempt to contact the study author with a one-page request listing the specific quantities needed and the deadline.
   - Third, exclude the study from the quantitative pool for that outcome but retain in the narrative summary; document the exclusion.
   - Optionally, pre-specify a single sensitivity-analysis imputation (e.g., borrow SD from comparable trials) and report results with and without it.

4.3. Document **author-contact tracking**: who is contacting, when, what was requested, what was received. The flow diagram should show how many studies contributed to each analysis after author contact.

4.4. For graphical data, pre-specify the **digitization rule**: which software, dual independent digitization, agreement threshold below which a third digitization is performed.

4.5. Pre-specify a **handling rule for studies with high attrition** in the primary outcome — typically a sensitivity analysis excluding studies with more than a stated attrition threshold.

### 5. Design the QC sampling

5.1. After independent extraction begins, plan a **random spot check** of a defined fraction of completed extractions by a third reviewer. Set the fraction based on team size and timeline (often 10–20% of studies, 100% of high-leverage trials).

5.2. Define what triggers an **expanded check**: a disagreement rate above a stated threshold in the calibration, a high-leverage trial (large weight in the pooled estimate), a methodologically complex trial (cluster design, multiple arms, adaptive design), or a trial with conflict of interest concerns.

5.3. Plan a **second-extraction-from-scratch** option for any high-leverage study where the original extraction is in dispute — both extractors begin again without seeing the first record.

5.4. Track QC findings in a log: study identifier, field, original value, corrected value, reason. This log becomes part of the deviations record.

### 6. Define the tooling and storage

6.1. Specify the extraction tool — a structured form in a dedicated review tool or a versioned spreadsheet with locked fields. Plain text is not sufficient for outcome data.

6.2. Specify the **version-control approach**: a frozen form definition with a date, and an explicit process for amending the form (numbered amendment, justification, retroactive re-extraction plan).

6.3. Specify the **storage location** for the raw data, the **access controls**, and the **retention plan**. The extracted data, with author-contact correspondence, must be reproducible and auditable.

6.4. Specify the **export format** for hand-off to the synthesis stage — a long-format table with one row per study-outcome-arm-time-point combination is the most flexible.

### 7. Plan the extraction-stage reporting

7.1. The flow diagram from the protocol gets updated with extraction-stage outputs: number of studies extracted, number of studies excluded at extraction (rare but possible — e.g., on discovering ineligibility during full extraction), number of studies contributing to each planned analysis after missing-data handling.

7.2. A **characteristics-of-included-studies table** is built from the extracted data — population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, follow-up, risk-of-bias summary per study. This is the report's anchor table and the readability bar for the rest of the methods.

7.3. A **reasons-for-exclusion table** at full-text extraction captures any studies that survived screening but fell out at extraction; the reasons here often surface ambiguities in the eligibility criteria worth documenting as protocol deviations.

### 8. Validate the plan against an internal checklist

Before emitting, verify:

- Every variable maps to a planned analysis, characteristics table, or risk-of-bias domain. Orphan variables are flagged for removal.
- Each outcome field has a primary metric, a secondary fallback metric with derivation rule, and an as-reported field.
- Multi-arm, cluster-randomized, and crossover handling rules exist for any design that the eligibility criteria admit.
- Dual extraction is specified for outcome and design-quality fields; calibration and conflict-resolution rules are explicit.
- Missing-data handling has rules for at least four common categories with derivation, author contact, and exclusion steps.
- Author-contact tracking is in the plan.
- QC sampling plan exists with trigger conditions and an expansion path.
- Tooling, version control, and storage are specified.
- Extraction-stage flow numbers are pre-defined for the final report.

### 9. Emit the extraction plan

Combine into a document with these headings: Purpose and Scope, Variable List, Outcome-Data Fields, Dual-Extraction Workflow, Conflict-Resolution Rules, Missing-Data Handling, QC Sampling, Tooling and Storage, Reporting at the Extraction Stage, Deviations Log Template. Append the `variable_table` as a sortable table.

### Decision rules and heuristics

- **Extract less, extract twice.** A short form extracted carefully by two reviewers beats a long form extracted hastily by one.
- **Capture the source.** Every numerical extraction needs a location (table number, figure number, supplement section). A field with no source is a field with no audit trail.
- **Derive but record.** When SDs are computed from CIs, store both the input CI and the derived SD with a flag indicating the derivation. Never overwrite the reported value with a derived one.
- **Pre-specify all imputations.** Post-hoc imputation rules look like data choices; pre-specified ones look like methods.
- **Author contact is methodology, not magic.** Plan it as a step with a deadline and a fallback, not as an optional courtesy.
- **One disagreement is data; many disagreements are a design problem.** If extractors keep disagreeing on the same field, the field definition is wrong.
- **Calibration before independence.** Independent extraction before calibration produces consistent disagreement, not consistent extraction.

### Edge cases

- **Trial reported in multiple publications.** Pre-specify the **primary report** (typically the main results paper) and the role of secondary reports (long-term follow-up, secondary outcomes, sub-studies). Extract from all and link to the primary record.
- **Data discrepancy across publications.** When the primary paper and a supplement disagree, extract both and surface the discrepancy as an `open_question` for the methodology lead.
- **Outcome reported only graphically.** Use dual-independent digitization with an agreement threshold; document the digitization tool and any smoothing.
- **Outcome reported only as p-value.** Reconstruct the effect estimate using the test statistic and degrees of freedom where possible; otherwise, mark the study as contributing to narrative synthesis only.
- **Conflict-of-interest signal.** If a study reports an unusual finding and is funded by a directly interested party, escalate to the methodology lead before downstream weighting decisions.
- **Subgroup data needed but not reported.** Contact authors as the first step; do not impute subgroup data from main-effect data.
- **Time-to-event outcome reported as median survival only.** Hazard ratio cannot be reliably reconstructed from medians alone; flag for author contact and, if unavailable, exclude from time-to-event meta-analysis.
- **Adaptive trial designs.** Extract the design rules and any adaptations made; the synthesis may need to model the design explicitly or exclude from primary analysis.

## Outputs

- `extraction_plan_markdown` — Full plan ready to attach to the protocol as the operational extraction guide.
- `variable_table` — Field-level reference for the extractors; usable as a starting point for the form definition in the review tool.
- `open_questions` — Items requiring methodology-lead sign-off before extraction begins.

## Examples

### Worked example (abbreviated)

Input:

> Review question: SGLT2 inhibitors vs. placebo in HFpEF adults without diabetes. Planned synthesis: random-effects meta-analysis of the composite cardiovascular outcome (CV death + HF hospitalization) at 12 months, with secondary outcomes (all-cause mortality, KCCQ change, serious AEs). Two extractors plus a methodologist. Included designs: parallel-group RCTs.

Expected `extraction_plan_markdown` (abbreviated):

```
Purpose: capture data sufficient to produce the pre-specified random-effects meta-analysis
  of the composite CV outcome and the secondary outcomes, with documented derivations and
  author-contact tracking.

Dual extraction: outcome data, randomization method, blinding, attrition by arm.
Single-with-check: study identification, declared funding, population descriptors.

Calibration: first 4 studies extracted by both reviewers; meet within 5 working days to
  reconcile and refine the form before independent extraction continues.

Conflict resolution: third-reviewer adjudication for outcome numerics; consensus
  discussion for design-judgment fields; default-to-paper rule for ambiguous wording with
  source location required.

Missing-data rules:
  - SD missing but CI reported: derive using the standard CI-to-SD transformation; record both.
  - SD missing but only p-value reported for a continuous outcome: contact author; if no response
    within 30 days, exclude from pooled estimate, retain in narrative.
  - Median + IQR reported instead of mean + SD: convert using a published transformation that
    matches the sample-size assumption; record the transformation used.
  - Composite outcome reported but components not separately reported at 12 months: contact author
    for component-level data; if unavailable, retain composite-only and note the limitation.

QC: 20% random sample of completed extractions checked by methodologist; expanded to 50% if the
  random sample disagreement rate exceeds 5% of outcome fields.

Tooling: dedicated review tool with field-level locking; frozen form definition v1.0 dated
  2026-06-01; amendments numbered and logged.

Reporting at the extraction stage: characteristics-of-included-studies table; per-outcome
  flow of studies contributing to each analysis after author contact and missing-data handling.
```

`variable_table` example (abbreviated):

```

| study_id         | text     | unique label per included study       | assigned          | Author-Year-CountryCode                              |
| design           | choice   | parallel_rct, cluster_rct, crossover  | Methods           | If not stated, contact authors; default parallel_rct |
| n_randomized_int | integer  | >= 1                                  | Methods/Table 1   | Total randomized to the intervention arm             |

```
- Confirm the time-point tolerance for the primary outcome (currently +/- 8 weeks).
- Decide on the IPD-not-IPD boundary: are we requesting individual participant data from

- Confirm the digitization tool for any graphical-only KCCQ data.

## Limitations

- The extraction plan describes what to capture, not how to interpret. Interpretation lives in the synthesis stage.
- The skill assumes that the protocol has settled the eligibility criteria; if eligibility is still drifting, the extraction plan will drift with it.
- The variable list is starting strong; teams should expect a pilot to surface fields to add or remove.
- The skill does not run extractions or compute derived statistics; it specifies the rules that human extractors and statistical software will apply.
- The skill does not replace risk-of-bias assessment, though it includes the design-quality fields that often live alongside the extraction form.
