# Clinical Risk Score Deployment Planner

> **Mandatory disclaimer.** This skill describes methodology for
> DESIGNING and planning the deployment of clinical decision support
> systems, including clinical risk scores. It is not itself a CDS,
> does not diagnose, does not recommend treatment, and is not a
> validation tool. Risk-score deployments in real clinical settings
> require qualified clinical informatics staff, biostatistical or
> data-science review, formal validation against clinical reference
> standards in the **local population**, regulatory clearance where
> applicable (e.g., FDA Software as a Medical Device, including the
> Predetermined Change Control Plan framework where relevant), and
> ongoing post-deployment surveillance. **No skill output may be
> deployed in care delivery without that full process.** Treat every
> artifact this skill produces as a governance-committee draft — not
> as a validation report, not as a regulatory submission, not as
> evidence of equity.

## When to use

Use this skill when a team wants to plan, or re-plan, the deployment of
a clinical risk score (sepsis early warning, readmission risk,
deterioration index, fall risk, mortality, suicide risk, etc.) into a
clinical workflow. Typical prompts:

- "We are considering deploying a published sepsis risk score in our
  inpatient EHR. Help us plan the validation and monitoring before
  go-live."
- "We already have a vendor-supplied risk score running silently.
  What does the audit before pilot look like?"
- "Draft a fairness audit plan for a readmission risk model."
- "Our deterioration index is two years old. Help us draft a
  drift-and-deprecation review."

Do **not** use this skill to:

- Fit, train, or tune a risk model. Use a qualified biostatistician /
  data scientist and an appropriate analysis environment.
- Choose specific thresholds or care actions to attach to a score.
- Produce a regulatory submission.
- Substitute for a fairness review with affected patient and
  community input.
- Replace the EHR vendor's clinical safety review for vendor-supplied
  scores.

## How to apply

Use the eight-phase plan below. Each phase ends with an artifact the
human team will take into governance.

### Phase 1 — Frame the clinical use case

Restate, in plain language:

- **Outcome** — exactly what the score predicts (e.g. in-hospital
  mortality within 24 hours, 30-day readmission, sepsis within 6
  hours). Be specific about the time horizon.
- **Population** — who is eligible (inpatient adults, ED visits,
  specific service lines, age range, exclusions).
- **Decision moment** — the workflow point at which the score is
  meant to *change behavior*. A score with no decision attached to
  it should not be deployed; it should stay in research.
- **Counter-factual** — what happens today, without the score?

Stop here if either of these is true:

- The decision moment is undefined.
- The counter-factual is "we already do the right thing reliably."

### Phase 2 — Review the evidence base

For each candidate score, gather and document:

- **Development cohort** — what population, what years, what setting.
- **Reported performance** — discrimination (e.g. AUROC),
  calibration (intercept and slope, or calibration plot), and any
  reported clinical-utility analysis (decision curve, net benefit).
- **External validation studies** — published validations in
  populations resembling yours.
- **Known failure modes** — populations or settings where the model
  is reported to underperform.
- **Equity evidence** — was performance reported by race, ethnicity,
  sex, age, language, insurance, geography? If not, treat that as a
  gap, not a clean bill of health.
- **Intervention evidence** — is there evidence that *acting on* the
  score improves outcomes, or only that the score is predictive?

Two principles enforced in the plan:

1. **Discrimination is not enough.** A well-discriminating model can
   be poorly calibrated, and a poorly calibrated model produces
   misleading thresholds.
2. **Predictive ≠ useful.** A score that is accurate but does not
   change behavior at the chosen threshold has no clinical value.

### Phase 3 — Plan local validation

Specify, in writing, before any deployment:

- **Local validation cohort** — definition (population, time
  window), size target, inclusion / exclusion logic mapped to FHIR
  resources (`Patient`, `Encounter`, `Observation`,
  `Condition`).
- **Outcome definition** — exact code lists or rules, with named
  owner. Outcome misclassification is the most common silent failure
  in risk-score validation.
- **Metrics**:
  - Discrimination (e.g. AUROC with CI).
  - Calibration (calibration intercept and slope; calibration plot
    in deciles; calibration-in-the-large).
  - Clinical utility at proposed thresholds (sensitivity,
    specificity, PPV, NPV, decision-curve net benefit).
  - **Subgroup analysis** — all metrics repeated for relevant
    subgroups. Pre-specify the subgroups; do not pick them after
    seeing results.
- **Sample-size justification** — calibration is the most
  sample-hungry property; document the assumed event rate and the
  minimum events per subgroup.
- **Pre-registration** — the validation plan is written, owner-signed,
  and dated **before** the analysis runs. Post-hoc threshold-shopping
  is a red flag in governance review.

### Phase 4 — Fairness audit

Independent of overall performance, document:

- **Subgroups in scope** — pre-specified. Typical: sex/gender, age
  bands, race and ethnicity (acknowledging that race is a social,
  not biological, variable), preferred language, insurance type /
  payer, primary care vs. no primary care, urban vs. rural.
- **Fairness metrics** — choose metrics that match the clinical use:
  - Equal calibration (calibration intercept and slope by subgroup).
  - Equal error rates at threshold (false-negative rate, false-positive
    rate by subgroup).
  - Equal utility (net benefit by subgroup).
- **Upstream bias review** — were the features and labels in the
  development cohort themselves products of biased care? (Example:
  prior healthcare utilization as a feature can encode access
  disparity.)
- **Mitigation options** if subgroup performance is uneven —
  re-calibration by subgroup, different thresholds by subgroup
  (clinically and legally fraught — escalate), feature changes,
  not deploying.
- **Affected-community input** — for any patient-facing or
  high-stakes use, document how patient and community perspectives
  are incorporated. The skill cannot perform that engagement; it
  surfaces the requirement.

**Fairness is not a checkbox.** Document residual concerns even when
metrics pass.

### Phase 5 — Workflow integration design

Use the companion `cds-feature-design` skill (or its checklist) to
specify:

- **Target user** — the single role who will see the score.
- **Surface** — non-interruptive banner, dashboard tile, CDS Hooks
  card, or SMART-on-FHIR app. Interruptive alerts driven by risk
  scores are a fatigue-fatigue red flag and require strong
  justification.
- **What is shown** — score value, risk band (low/medium/high),
  trend, and crucially the **recommended action** at the chosen
  threshold. A score without a recommended action invites
  inconsistent practice.
- **Trigger event** — when the score recomputes and re-surfaces.
- **Suppression rules** — already addressed, on comfort care,
  already in higher-acuity setting, already on monitored bed.
- **Override workflow** — closed-list override reasons, audit log,
  re-presentation rules.

### Phase 6 — Silent (shadow) deployment

Before any clinical surface:

- **Duration** — typically a minimum of 30–90 days, longer for low
  event-rate outcomes.
- **What is logged** — score values, eligibility status, would-be
  firings, and the actual clinical outcome.
- **Stability checks** — daily firing-rate stability, comparison of
  observed performance to Phase 3 expected performance, subgroup
  parity stability.
- **Failure stop** — predefined criteria that abort live deployment
  (e.g. calibration intercept outside `<RANGE>`, AUROC below
  `<THRESHOLD>` in any pre-specified subgroup, observed event rate
  outside `<RANGE>` of expected).

### Phase 7 — Pilot, then monitor

For pilot:

- **Scope** — one unit / one service line / one shift pattern.
- **Concurrent measurement** — Phase 7.1 metrics:
  - Score-level: firing rate, override rate, time-to-action.
  - Clinical: the intended outcome metric, plus balancing measures
    (e.g. for a sepsis score: rate of antibiotic over-treatment,
    rate of unnecessary lactate draws, ICU transfer rate).
  - Equity slice on all of the above.
- **Roll-back trigger** — at least one explicit, pre-specified
  trigger that ends the pilot early.

For ongoing monitoring (post-pilot):

- **Calibration monitoring** — recurring (monthly or quarterly)
  calibration intercept-and-slope check against the most recent
  N encounters.
- **Drift detection** — population drift (input feature
  distributions), label drift (outcome rates), and performance
  drift (discrimination and calibration). Drift requires a
  pre-specified review, not an automated retrain.
- **Equity monitoring** — recurring subgroup performance review on
  the same cadence.
- **Incident pathway** — how does a clinician report concern about a
  specific score firing, and what happens within `<SLA>`?

### Phase 8 — Deprecation and change control

Pre-specify, in writing:

- **Sunset triggers** — calibration drift beyond `<THRESHOLD>` for
  `<DURATION>`; new published evidence contradicts use; underlying
  data feed changes (e.g. lab method change, vital-sign device
  change); guideline change.
- **Vendor-update handling** — if the score is vendor-supplied,
  what happens when the vendor updates the model? A model update is
  a new deployment and triggers re-validation, not silent rollover.
  This is particularly important under regulatory frameworks that
  treat algorithm change control as a regulated activity (e.g.
  FDA's predetermined-change-control-plan concept). The skill
  flags this; it does not interpret regulatory status.
- **Decommission plan** — communication to clinicians, removal from
  workflow surfaces, archival of monitoring data, governance sign-off.
- **Post-deprecation surveillance** — short window (e.g. 30–90 days)
  after removal to detect unintended effects (e.g. delayed
  escalation if the score had become embedded in implicit
  workflow).

## Limitations

- The skill plans deployment; it does **not** fit models, compute
  metrics, run audits, or access patient data.
- The skill cannot validate the published evidence for any specific
  score; it can only structure how the team examines that evidence.
- Fairness is treated as a structured set of questions, not as a
  solved problem. Real fairness work requires affected-community
  engagement, qualitative review, and ongoing recalibration.
- Regulatory interpretation (FDA SaMD scope, EU MDR class,
  predetermined-change-control eligibility, local equivalents) is
  out of scope; the skill surfaces the question and recommends
  formal review.
- The skill does not recommend specific thresholds. Threshold
  choice depends on local cost / benefit and clinical context and
  is a governance decision.
- The skill does not handle implementation of the model itself; it
  assumes the team uses appropriate analysis tooling under
  qualified supervision.
- Multi-site or federated deployments add governance, data, and
  recalibration complexities that are only partially covered here.
