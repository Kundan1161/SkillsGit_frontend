# Support Ticket Triager

## When to use

Invoke this skill when a raw, unsorted customer message lands in front of you and the next decision is "what is this, how urgent, who handles it, have we seen it before, and what do we say first." Typical entry points are an email forwarded from a shared inbox, a fresh row in a help-desk queue, a transcript pasted from chat, a feedback widget submission, an app-store review that escalated, a Twitter or LinkedIn DM that needs to be folded back into the formal queue, or a community-forum post a moderator flagged.

Do not invoke this skill when the work is already past intake — for example, when a ticket has been triaged once and you are now actually drafting the substantive response, building a knowledge-base article from the resolution, or running a postmortem on a multi-day outage. For those, reach for a reply-drafting or KB-article skill instead. This skill is purely the doorway: the first ninety seconds after a message arrives.

The skill also fits cleanly into batch workflows. If you have a backlog of fifty messages that piled up overnight, run them through this skill one by one (or in parallel) to get a sortable, color-coded queue that surfaces the few that genuinely need a human at 8:01 AM and lets the rest wait for the standard rotation.

## How to apply

The agent should treat triage as a small pipeline of decisions, each gated by evidence rather than vibes. Follow the steps in order — later steps depend on earlier ones — and do not skip ahead even if the answer feels obvious.

1. **Read the ticket end-to-end before deciding anything.** Many tickets bury the actual problem in paragraph three. Skim for the question, the symptom, and the emotional register. If the message is long and rambling, extract the load-bearing sentences into a one-sentence summary in your scratch space; do not output it yet.

2. **Detect the language.** If it is not the team's working language, set a `language` field and prefer the customer's language for the draft acknowledgment. A reply in the wrong language at intake is a relationship-damaging first impression that the rest of the pipeline cannot undo.

3. **Classify into one primary category.** Use a small, mutually-exclusive set the agent can be precise about:
   - **bug** — something the customer says used to work or should obviously work, and does not.
   - **outage** — bug-like, but the customer or wording implies many users are affected, or there is a visible status indicator.
   - **how-to** — the customer is asking how to accomplish a task in the product, not reporting a defect.
   - **feature-request** — they want a capability that does not exist.
   - **billing** — invoices, charges, refunds, plan changes, tax, payment methods.
   - **account** — login, password reset, seat management, ownership transfer, deletion.
   - **abuse-or-security** — credential leak, harassment report, exposed data, suspicious activity.
   - **feedback** — opinion, praise, complaint without a specific defect.
   - **partnership-sales** — business development or commercial inquiries that arrived in the support channel by mistake.
   - **spam** — automated, off-topic, or trivially irrelevant.

   Pick exactly one. If the message contains two genuinely separable problems (e.g. a billing question plus a bug report), still pick one primary and surface the secondary as `also_contains` in the JSON. A second ticket gets opened downstream.

4. **Assign a subcategory.** Subcategories are softer and team-specific. Suggested defaults: under bug — `data-loss`, `regression`, `ui-glitch`, `integration`, `performance`, `crash`. Under billing — `refund`, `failed-charge`, `plan-change`, `tax-invoice`, `seat-mgmt`. Under account — `login`, `password-reset`, `mfa`, `ownership`, `deletion`. Under how-to — `setup`, `feature-usage`, `migration`, `api`. These map cleanly to skill-based routing later.

5. **Read the customer context.** If `customer_context` was provided, factor in plan tier, MRR, lifetime tickets, and any recent product-usage flags. A trial user reporting a UI glitch is not the same priority as an enterprise customer reporting the same glitch on day one of a paid pilot. Be honest with yourself about this — pretending tier-blindness produces SLAs that the business actually breaches.

6. **Detect escalation signals.** Skim the message specifically for: legal language ("attorney," "GDPR," "subpoena"), public-channel threats ("posting to Twitter," "App Store review"), churn signals ("canceling," "moving to competitor"), data-loss claims, security-incident language ("leaked," "exposed," "breach"), and severe emotional distress. These do not all auto-elevate to P1, but they all belong in the JSON output as `escalation_signals` so a human reviewer can decide.

7. **Compute priority.** Use the impact-x-urgency matrix the team picked in `priority_policy` (default: strict_p1_p4). The defaults the agent should fall back to when policy is unspecified:
   - **P1 — Critical.** Outage, data loss, security incident, or a paying customer fully blocked from core functionality with no workaround. First response within 15 minutes; target resolution within 4 hours.
   - **P2 — High.** Significant degradation affecting a paying customer's daily work; a workaround may exist but is painful. First response within 1 hour; target resolution within one business day.
   - **P3 — Normal.** Standard bug, how-to, billing question, feature request from an existing customer. First response within 4 business hours; target resolution within three business days.
   - **P4 — Low.** Cosmetic issues, "nice to have" feedback, questions from non-paying users with no urgency. First response within one business day; resolution best-effort.

   If `priority_policy` is `b2b_enterprise`, tighten P1 to 5-minute response and add a `P0` for cross-customer outages. If `b2c_consumer`, loosen P1 to one hour and drop SLAs on P4 entirely. Always emit the numeric minutes and hours alongside the label so downstream systems do not have to re-interpret.

8. **Route to the owning team.** Use `routing_map` if provided. If not, fall back to a generic table: bug-frontend → frontend-eng, bug-backend → backend-eng, bug-integration → integrations, billing → finance-support, account → identity-support, abuse-or-security → trust-and-safety, partnership-sales → sales-ops, feature-request → product-research, feedback → cx-management. Spam is dropped, not routed. Output the routing target as a stable identifier the help-desk system can resolve.

9. **Check for duplicates against `known_issues`.** Compare the ticket's symptom and context to each known-issue summary. Be conservative — duplicate-tagging the wrong ticket sends real customers into a black hole. A duplicate match requires either (a) the symptom matches an active incident summary line-for-line on the load-bearing nouns and verbs, or (b) the error message or stack-trace fingerprint is identical. Vague similarity ("also a billing issue") does not count. When in doubt, do not mark it a duplicate; flag a `possibly_related_to` instead.

10. **Estimate sentiment.** Three buckets is plenty: `positive`, `neutral`, `frustrated`. A fourth `severely-distressed` is reserved for messages that mention real-world harm, self-harm, severe financial loss, or rage with personal threats. The acknowledgment draft must adapt to sentiment — see step 13.

11. **Suggest labels.** Two to five short tags the help-desk can apply automatically: the category, the subcategory, the product surface area (e.g. `mobile-ios`, `web-dashboard`, `api`), and any unusually specific signal (e.g. `payment-3ds`, `seat-overage`). Avoid label inflation — every label costs a future agent reading the queue.

12. **Compute confidence.** A single float between 0.0 and 1.0 for the primary category, plus a brief one-line justification. Confidence below 0.6 should automatically suggest a human review step. The agent should be honest about ambiguity rather than picking a category just to look decisive.

13. **Draft the acknowledgment.** This is the customer-facing artifact. Rules:
    - One to four short paragraphs. No long openings, no "we have received your request" boilerplate that adds nothing.
    - Address the person by name if a name was extractable.
    - State explicitly that the message was received, that it is being looked into, and what happens next (who, when, how).
    - Match the sentiment. For `frustrated`, acknowledge the inconvenience plainly without grovelling; for `severely-distressed`, acknowledge the severity, avoid happy-talk, and route to a human escalation path inside the text.
    - Do not promise resolution times the SLA does not commit to. Use the SLA-derived window phrased in plain English: "you'll hear back from a teammate within four business hours."
    - Do not include internal labels, priority codes, or routing targets in the customer-facing text.
    - If the duplicate check matched a known incident, link to the status page or canonical incident-update channel rather than re-explaining the issue.
    - If the category is `spam` or `partnership-sales`, do not draft an acknowledgment — emit a note that no reply is needed and explain why.

14. **Assemble the outputs.** The triage_report (markdown) is for the human triager glancing at the queue: short, scannable, with the draft at the bottom. The triage_json is for systems: stable field names, no prose smuggled into enum slots, ISO-8601 timestamps if you compute SLA deadlines from a reference time.

15. **Self-check before returning.** Run three quick consistency checks:
    - Category and routing target agree (a billing ticket does not route to backend-eng).
    - Priority and SLA minutes/hours agree with the policy table.
    - The draft acknowledgment does not contradict the duplicate finding (do not say "we are investigating your unique report" if you tagged it as duplicate of an active incident).
    If any check fails, fix and re-emit. Do not return inconsistent output and ask the human to reconcile.

16. **When ambiguity is irreducible, say so.** A message that says "Hi, please call me" with no further detail is genuinely un-triageable; do not invent a category. Return category `needs-clarification`, priority `P3` by default, and an acknowledgment that asks the smallest number of high-leverage questions to unblock the next pass.

## Inputs

- `ticket_text` (required) — the raw customer message. Email signatures, quoted prior threads, and forwarding headers should be stripped before analysis but kept in scratch in case sender identity is needed.
- `customer_context` (optional, JSON) — plan tier, MRR, signup date, lifetime ticket count, last-active date, product-usage flags. Even partial context (just plan tier) materially improves priority calibration.
- `known_issues` (optional, text) — a synced summary of currently-tracked incidents and their canonical IDs. Treat anything older than seven days as stale unless explicitly marked active.
- `routing_map` (optional, text) — team-to-category mapping. If omitted, the agent falls back to the generic table in step 8.
- `priority_policy` (optional, choice) — which SLA preset to apply. See step 7.
- `tone` (optional, choice) — voice of the acknowledgment draft. Defaults to warm-but-professional.

## Outputs

- `triage_report` (markdown) — human-readable summary, ordered: one-line ticket summary, category and subcategory, priority and SLA window, owning team, duplicate finding, escalation signals, suggested labels, then the draft acknowledgment under its own heading.
- `triage_json` (JSON) — fields: `category`, `subcategory`, `also_contains`, `priority`, `priority_policy`, `sla_first_response_minutes`, `sla_resolve_hours`, `owning_team`, `duplicate_of`, `possibly_related_to`, `sentiment`, `language`, `suggested_labels`, `escalation_signals`, `confidence`, `confidence_reason`, `needs_human_review`.

## Examples

### Example 1 — bug report from a paying SMB customer

**Input ticket_text:**

> Subject: Reports won't export — losing my mind here
>
> Hey, I've been trying to export my Q1 financial report as PDF for an hour. Every time I hit Export, the button spins for like 30 seconds and then nothing happens. No download, no error message, nothing. I have a board meeting in 90 minutes and I genuinely need this report. I'm on the Growth plan. Help?
>
> — Priya

**Input customer_context:** `{ "plan": "growth", "mrr": 199, "lifetime_tickets": 3, "last_active": "2026-05-14" }`

**Input known_issues:** `INC-441 — PDF export silently failing for Growth-plan users on Chrome 129+. Workaround: CSV export then convert. Active since 2026-05-13.`

**Output triage_report (excerpt):**

```
Summary: PDF export silently fails; customer is time-pressured (board meeting in 90m).
Category: bug / export
Priority: P2 — first response within 1 hour, resolve within 1 business day
Owning team: frontend-eng
Duplicate of: INC-441 (active, has workaround)
Sentiment: frustrated
Labels: bug, export, pdf, web-dashboard, time-sensitive

--- Draft acknowledgment ---

Hi Priya,

I'm sorry — losing an export an hour before a board meeting is the worst kind of timing. I've found your report: this is a known issue we've been tracking since yesterday (INC-441), and CSV export still works as a stopgap, so you can pull the numbers that way and drop them into your slides while we finish the fix.

A teammate from our frontend team will follow up within the hour with a status update and an ETA. If you need a hand walking through the CSV workaround in the meantime, just reply here.

— Support
```

**Output triage_json:**

```json
{
  "category": "bug",
  "subcategory": "export",
  "priority": "P2",
  "priority_policy": "strict_p1_p4",
  "sla_first_response_minutes": 60,
  "sla_resolve_hours": 8,
  "owning_team": "frontend-eng",
  "duplicate_of": "INC-441",
  "sentiment": "frustrated",
  "language": "en",
  "suggested_labels": ["bug", "export", "pdf", "web-dashboard", "time-sensitive"],
  "escalation_signals": ["time-pressured"],
  "confidence": 0.92,
  "needs_human_review": false
}
```

### Example 2 — vague feedback that triggers needs-clarification

**Input ticket_text:**

> hi the new dashboard is bad

**Input customer_context:** `{ "plan": "free", "mrr": 0, "lifetime_tickets": 0 }`

**Output triage_report (excerpt):**

```
Summary: Negative sentiment about the redesigned dashboard, no specific defect cited.
Category: feedback (with needs-clarification flag)
Priority: P4
Owning team: cx-management
Duplicate of: none
Sentiment: frustrated
Labels: feedback, dashboard-redesign

--- Draft acknowledgment ---

Hi — thanks for telling us, and sorry the new dashboard isn't working for you. So we can actually do something about it, could you share one or two specifics? For example: is there a thing you used to find easily that's harder to find now, or a particular screen that feels broken? Even a screenshot helps a lot.

— Support
```

### Example 3 — security-flavored escalation

**Input ticket_text:**

> I just got an email saying my password was changed but I didn't change it. I'm locked out of my account and my company's data is in there. Please help immediately. This is urgent.

**Output triage_report (excerpt):**

```
Summary: Suspected unauthorized account access; customer locked out; sensitive business data implicated.
Category: abuse-or-security / account-takeover
Priority: P1 — first response within 15 minutes
Owning team: trust-and-safety
Duplicate of: none
Sentiment: severely-distressed
Escalation signals: security-incident, account-lockout, business-data-implicated
Labels: security, account-takeover, urgent
Needs human review: yes (low automation tolerance for security flow)
```

The draft acknowledgment for this category is intentionally short and routes the customer into the human security flow without making promises about recovery timelines that the team has not yet validated.

## Limitations

- Triage quality is bounded by the freshness of `known_issues`. A skill run against a stale incident summary will under-detect duplicates and waste customer time. Treat anything older than a week as stale and prefer "possibly related" over "duplicate of."
- Customer context is materially load-bearing. Without plan-tier or MRR signals, the priority decision degrades to symptom-only severity, which over-prioritizes loud-but-low-value tickets and under-prioritizes enterprise pain that arrived politely.
- The skill does not access the customer's product data, account history, or prior tickets directly. If the team requires per-ticket pull of account state, that fetch is the caller's responsibility — the skill works on what it is handed.
- Sentiment detection is coarse by design. It is reliable enough to color the acknowledgment but not granular enough to drive downstream behavior on its own. Do not gate refunds or account credits on this skill's sentiment label.
- Bias caution: priority calibration uses plan tier when provided, which is correct for SLA purposes but should not be confused with empathy. The acknowledgment tone is set by sentiment, not tier; a free-tier user in real distress gets a careful reply, not a perfunctory one.
- Languages outside the team's working set are detected and surfaced but not translated. The acknowledgment will be drafted in the detected language only if the agent has confidence in producing native-quality prose; otherwise it falls back to English and flags the language for human follow-up.
