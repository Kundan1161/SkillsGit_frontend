# Audit PBC Fulfiller

## When to use

Use this skill when a company is in the middle of, or about to start, an external audit and needs to operationalize the auditor's Prepared-by-Client (PBC) request list. The PBC list is the formal set of documents, schedules, and evidence the auditor expects the company to provide. Done poorly, the PBC becomes a fire drill that exhausts the accounting team, delays the audit, and produces low-quality evidence that triggers additional auditor questions. Done well, the PBC is a project plan: each item has an owner, a deadline, a defined evidence package, and a review step before delivery.

The skill applies to multiple audit families. The most common is the annual financial-statement audit (10-K, regulator-filed financials, lender-required audited statements). The skill also applies to SOC 1 (controls over financial reporting at service organizations), SOC 2 (security/availability controls), SOX ITGC and process testing (controls audits for public companies), internal audit cycles, tax audits, and regulatory audits. The PBC mechanics overlap heavily across these — what differs is the depth of evidence and the specific request types.

It applies whether the user is the controller running point on a first-time audit, an audit liaison at a multi-entity company orchestrating dozens of PBC owners, or an internal-audit lead responding to a sister team's testing program. The skill is appropriate during PBC receipt (designing the response plan), during fieldwork (executing against the plan, handling auditor follow-ups), and during wrap-up (final deliverables, management representation letters, sign-off).

Do not use this skill to dispute audit findings — those require domain-specific accounting judgment plus negotiation. Do not use it to design the company's controls in the first place — that lives in operational-controls and SOX design work. Do not use it to write the audit opinion — that is the auditor's deliverable, not the client's.

The skill is opinionated about three things. First: the PBC is a project, not a list — every item has an owner, a deadline, and a status that updates daily. Second: every evidence package is reviewed by a second person before it leaves the company; raw evidence sent to auditors generates follow-up questions, while reviewed evidence answers questions in advance. Third: sampling responses follow the auditor's selection deterministically — improvised "we'll send what we have" responses are the slowest path to a deficient audit.

## How to apply

The skill runs a deterministic nine-stage pipeline. Each stage produces a fragment of the response package.

### Stage 1 — Parse the PBC list

1. Read the PBC list. Most lists are structured loosely; the skill normalizes every item into a record with the following fields:
   - **id** — a stable identifier; if the auditor provided one, keep it; otherwise assign sequential ids.
   - **section** — the audit area (revenue, AP, cash, payroll, inventory, fixed assets, equity, controls, IT general controls, etc.).
   - **description** — what the auditor is asking for.
   - **type** — request type (see Stage 2).
   - **auditor_owner** — the auditor team member who owns the request (if specified).
   - **due_date** — the auditor's requested delivery date.
   - **company_owner** — to be assigned in Stage 3.
   - **status** — initial value "received."
2. Group items by section. Sectional grouping reveals workload distribution and identifies the biggest "blast radius" sections that warrant more attention.
3. Identify duplicates and near-duplicates. Auditors often ask for the same evidence under different framings (the trial balance appears under "general," "revenue," and "balance sheet"). Mark duplicates so they map to a single response package.
4. Flag ambiguous items. If the auditor's description does not make the request clear, the item gets status "awaiting-clarification" and an auditor-callback is the first action.

### Stage 2 — Classify the request types

5. Every PBC item is one of a handful of types. The type determines the response shape:
   - **Document request.** A specific contract, agreement, or memo. Response: locate, redact if necessary, package, deliver.
   - **Schedule request.** A schedule the company prepares (revenue waterfall, AR aging, fixed asset roll-forward, deferred revenue schedule). Response: export from the ERP or prepare from sub-ledger, review for accuracy.
   - **Reconciliation request.** A reconciliation the auditor wants to see. Response: provide the completed rec workpaper from the close (see `reconciliation-builder` skill).
   - **Sample request.** A sample of transactions for substantive testing. Response: pull the underlying source documents for each sampled item (invoice, contract, approval, payment evidence).
   - **Walkthrough request.** A narrative description of a process plus the controls in it. Response: prepare a process narrative, identify controls, gather evidence of control operation.
   - **Inquiry / interview.** A discussion with named personnel. Response: schedule, prepare key talking points, post-meeting summary memo.
   - **Confirmation.** Auditor sends a request directly to a third party (bank, customer, vendor, attorney) but requires the company to authorize and supply the contact. Response: provide contact list, authorize the auditor's confirmation letter.
   - **Management representation.** A letter from management asserting specific facts. Response: draft, route through executive sign-off.
   - **System access / extraction.** Read-only access to ERP or evidence systems. Response: provision per IT security review, monitor access.

### Stage 3 — Assign owners

6. Map each PBC item to an internal owner role first, then to a person if `company_team` was supplied. Default mapping:
   - Revenue items: revenue accountant / controller.
   - AP items: AP manager / staff accountant.
   - Cash and treasury: treasury accountant / controller.
   - Payroll: payroll accountant / HR-finance liaison.
   - Inventory: inventory accountant / operations finance.
   - Fixed assets: GL accountant assigned to fixed assets.
   - Tax: tax manager / external tax provider.
   - Equity / stock comp: financial reporting accountant / equity administrator.
   - Process narratives and walkthroughs: process owner (accounting, sales ops, HR, IT, depending on the cycle).
   - ITGC: IT manager / DevOps / Security lead.
   - Management representation letter: CFO / Controller.
   - Confirmations: AR or AP lead depending on direction.
7. Avoid concentrating ownership on a single person. If the controller ends up as owner for more than ~25% of items, the audit will block on the controller; redistribute.
8. Assign a backup owner for every item. Audits run multi-week; people take vacation, get sick. The backup is the first point of contact if the primary is unavailable.
9. Assign an audit liaison: a single person who owns the relationship with the auditor, fields all incoming questions, and routes them. The liaison is usually the controller or an assistant controller. The liaison is not the owner of any single item — they orchestrate the responders.

### Stage 4 — Set internal due dates

10. The auditor's due date is the external deadline. Set the company's internal due date 2-3 business days earlier so the response can be reviewed before transmission.
11. Sequence items by dependency. Schedule requests may depend on the close being final; sample requests may depend on the auditor's selection (which they typically send during fieldwork). The skill identifies the dependency chain so the team is not blocking on something that the auditor has not yet asked for.
12. Highlight the critical path: items that, if delayed, slip the audit timeline. Cash confirmations are usually on the critical path because they require third-party response time. Sample testing items are on the critical path because the auditor cannot start substantive testing without them.
13. Cluster items with the same owner into batches with shared review windows. Reviewing one schedule and then another in the same session is more efficient than ping-pong.

### Stage 5 — Define evidence packaging standards

14. Every response is a package, not a loose document. Default package contents:
    - **Cover memo.** One paragraph: what the auditor asked, what the package contains, what assumptions or scope limitations apply.
    - **The evidence itself.** Schedule, document, screenshot, or extract.
    - **Reconciliation to source.** When the evidence is a schedule, show how it ties to the trial balance or the underlying system.
    - **Reviewer attestation.** Initials and date of the second-person review.
15. Naming convention: every package file follows `PBC-{id}_{section}_{short-description}_{period}_{version}.pdf` so the auditor can find what they're looking at and the company can find what was sent. Versioning matters because auditors will request revisions.
16. Redaction policy: redact PII that is not necessary for the audit (customer addresses, employee SSNs beyond last-4) before transmission. The audit liaison approves redaction before transmission.
17. Delivery channel: use the auditor's portal (most large auditors have one) for confidentiality and version control. Email transmission is acceptable for small packages but every email-delivered package is also logged in the tracker.

### Stage 6 — Sample response protocol

18. Sample requests are the most common audit deliverable and the most commonly botched. When the auditor sends a sample selection:
    - Acknowledge the selection within 24 hours.
    - For each sampled item, the responder pulls the underlying source documents per the auditor's stated evidence list. Default evidence per transaction type:
      - Revenue sample: contract or order, invoice, shipping or delivery evidence, customer receipt or acceptance, cash receipt evidence.
      - AP sample: invoice, PO, receiving document, approval, payment evidence.
      - Payroll sample: offer letter or rate authorization, time records, payroll register, payment evidence.
      - Cash receipt sample: source of receipt (customer remittance, ACH detail), deposit confirmation, GL posting.
      - Cash disbursement sample: invoice or expense report, approval, payment evidence.
      - Journal entry sample: entry, supporting calculation, approval evidence.
    - Package per sampled item — one folder per item, contents per the evidence list, plus the cover memo.
    - Reviewer reviews each folder for completeness before transmission.
19. If a sampled transaction has missing source documents, do not improvise a substitute. Flag it, note what is missing, document why, and route to the audit liaison. Improvised substitutes are how minor evidence gaps escalate to control deficiency findings.
20. Track sample-response completeness in the tracker: a sample with 25 items has 25 sub-tasks; each is independently complete or not.

### Stage 7 — Walkthrough preparation

21. A walkthrough is the auditor's narrative-based test of a process and its controls. Prepare:
    - **Process narrative.** A two-to-five page document describing the end-to-end process, the inputs, the steps, the systems, the outputs. The narrative is the auditor's roadmap.
    - **Control identification.** Within the narrative, mark the controls — both preventive (approval-before-posting) and detective (reconciliation-after-the-fact).
    - **Control evidence.** For each control, prepare evidence of operation: a sample showing the approval, a screenshot of the system enforcement, a copy of the reconciliation.
    - **Personnel availability.** The walkthrough usually involves 2-4 process participants (the preparer, the reviewer, the system administrator). Schedule them with buffers.
22. Pre-walk the walkthrough internally before meeting the auditor. The audit liaison runs the walkthrough with the participants in advance so gaps are surfaced without the auditor present.
23. During the walkthrough with the auditor, the participants speak; the audit liaison observes and notes follow-ups. Send a follow-up memo within 24 hours summarizing what was discussed and what additional evidence was promised.

### Stage 8 — Tracker construction

24. The tracker is the operational document for the audit. Structure:
    - **Item id** — stable across the audit.
    - **Section** — audit area.
    - **Description** — what is requested.
    - **Type** — from Stage 2.
    - **Auditor owner** — auditor team member.
    - **Company owner** — primary.
    - **Backup owner** — secondary.
    - **Auditor due date** — the formal deadline.
    - **Internal due date** — 2-3 days earlier than auditor due date.
    - **Status** — see Stage 9 status taxonomy.
    - **Evidence reference** — link or path to the response package once prepared.
    - **Reviewer** — who attested the package.
    - **Transmission date** — when sent to auditor.
    - **Notes** — open questions, follow-ups, blockers.
25. The tracker is reviewed daily by the audit liaison. Open items aged past their internal due date escalate immediately to the owner and to the controller if owner is non-responsive.
26. The tracker is the single source of truth. Email side-channels about PBC items get summarized into the tracker; nothing lives only in email.

### Stage 9 — Status taxonomy and escalation

27. Standard status values for any PBC item:
    - **Received** — added to tracker, not yet started.
    - **Clarification needed** — pending auditor callback.
    - **In progress** — owner is preparing.
    - **Prepared** — owner has prepared package, pending reviewer.
    - **In review** — reviewer is checking.
    - **Reviewed** — passed review, awaiting transmission.
    - **Transmitted** — sent to auditor.
    - **Follow-up requested** — auditor asked for more.
    - **Closed** — auditor confirms acceptance.
28. Escalation rules:
    - Status "received" for 5+ business days with no movement: owner contacted by audit liaison.
    - Status "in progress" for 7+ business days: owner provides status update; audit liaison reviews scope and reassigns if needed.
    - Internal due date missed by 1 day: controller awareness.
    - Internal due date missed by 3+ days: CFO awareness if material item.
    - "Follow-up requested" open for 5+ business days: same escalation as "received."
29. Daily standup during fieldwork: a 15-minute meeting of the audit liaison plus the controllers running each section, focused only on status changes and blockers since yesterday. The meeting reads the tracker; it does not re-create the tracker.

### Stage 10 — Quality bar before transmission

30. Every package must pass a quality check before going to the auditor:
    - The package answers the auditor's specific question (re-read the PBC description).
    - The package is internally consistent (totals tie, schedules reconcile, references resolve).
    - The cover memo is present and short.
    - Names match the auditor's vocabulary (e.g., "trade receivables" vs. "accounts receivable" — match what the auditor used in the request).
    - PII is redacted appropriately.
    - File names follow the convention.
31. The reviewer's job is not to redo the package; it is to attest that the package answers the question and is internally consistent. A reviewer who finds a defect routes back to the preparer; the reviewer does not silently fix and forward.
32. The audit liaison spot-checks ~10-20% of packages at random before transmission, biasing the sample toward high-risk sections (revenue, complex estimates, items related to prior audit findings).

### Stage 11 — Response to auditor follow-ups

33. When the auditor responds to a delivered package with a follow-up question, treat the follow-up as a new tracker item (or as a sub-item on the original). Respond within 2 business days unless the question is materially complex.
34. Follow-up question types:
    - **Clarification.** The auditor needs to understand something in the package. Response: a one-paragraph email or memo; usually does not require a new package.
    - **Additional evidence.** The auditor needs more support. Response: a new package referencing the prior delivery's id.
    - **Reconciliation challenge.** The auditor finds something that does not tie. Response: a written reconciliation showing how the items reconcile, or a correction and acknowledgement.
    - **Methodology challenge.** The auditor questions a calculation or accounting position. Response: a position memo with supporting authoritative literature references. Escalate to the controller or CFO before responding.
35. Never argue with the auditor in writing about a methodology challenge in the first round. Acknowledge the question, schedule a discussion, prepare the position memo for the discussion. Written arguments harden into deliverables; conversational arguments preserve room to refine the position.

### Stage 12 — Management representation letter

36. The management representation letter is signed at the end of fieldwork by the CEO and CFO. It asserts facts about the financial statements and the company's controls. The auditor typically provides a draft; the company reviews, negotiates specific assertions if necessary, and signs.
37. The rep letter is reviewed by the CFO, the controller, and legal counsel before signature. Each line is checked against the company's records: do the assertions match what the company actually represented during the audit?
38. The rep letter is dated as of the auditor's report date. The audit cannot conclude without it.

### Stage 13 — Closing the audit

39. At the end of fieldwork, the audit liaison delivers a closing memo to the controller: PBC items completed, items still open and their disposition, audit findings (if any), management responses to findings.
40. The audit liaison preserves the audit response file — every package, every email, the tracker history — for the company's records and for the next year's audit. Repeat audits benefit massively from prior-year continuity.
41. Conduct a post-audit retrospective: what went well, what slowed things down, which items consistently caused delays, which roles need different support next year. The retrospective is the input to next year's audit prep.

## Patterns the skill applies

### Pattern A — The owner-not-role rule

Assign individuals, not committees, as owners. "AP team" is not an owner; "Sarah, AP manager" is. Diffuse ownership produces diffuse responsibility. When `company_team` is supplied, owners are people; when it is not, owners are roles, and the user is asked to map roles to people before fieldwork begins.

### Pattern B — Reviewer separation

The preparer and reviewer are different humans. For small teams, this can mean the controller reviews the staff accountant's package, and the controller's own packages are reviewed by another senior staff member or by the CFO. Without separation, the audit liaison reviews; without that, packages do not transmit.

### Pattern C — The two-day buffer

Internal due dates lead auditor due dates by 2-3 business days. The buffer absorbs late items, allows reviewer turn, and creates room for a re-do. Without buffer, every delay becomes the auditor's delay.

### Pattern D — Front-loading the heavy items

The skill front-loads the heaviest items into the early days of fieldwork: large schedules, complex reconciliations, items that depend on the close being final. Lighter items (single-document fetches, simple confirmations) are interleaved with the heavy work to keep momentum without overloading.

### Pattern E — Prior-year leverage

Items the auditor requested last year are likely to repeat. The skill pre-builds the response packages for likely-repeat items in advance of receiving the current PBC, then maps incoming requests to pre-built packages. This shortens the response window dramatically.

### Pattern F — Auditor-vocabulary mirroring

When responding, use the words the auditor used in the request. If they asked about "trade receivables," do not respond with a schedule labeled "customer balances" without a translation note. Vocabulary mismatch generates follow-up questions that are pure waste.

### Pattern G — Position memos for hard items

Any item the auditor is likely to challenge (revenue recognition for complex contracts, impairment indicators, estimate methodologies) gets a position memo prepared in advance — before the auditor asks. The memo cites the authoritative literature, describes the company's policy, and shows the application. Position memos pre-empt 80% of auditor challenges on hard items.

### Pattern H — Follow-up batching

Auditor follow-ups arrive throughout fieldwork. Batch responses into one or two daily slots rather than thrashing on each as it arrives. The thrashing cost (context switch) is real; the small delay (a few hours) costs almost nothing.

## Outputs

The skill returns three artifacts:

1. `pbc_tracker` — the structured tracker, in markdown, that the audit liaison runs the audit from.
2. `response_playbook` — how to respond to each request type, including sampling, walkthroughs, follow-ups, and management representations.
3. `tracker_json` — the tracker in machine-readable form so it can be imported into a project tool.

## Limitations

- The skill does not prepare the underlying accounting; it organizes the response to an audit. If the underlying close is incomplete or contains errors, the PBC response will surface those — the response process cannot heal them.
- The skill does not negotiate the audit timeline or scope with the auditor; it assumes the timeline as input. If the timeline is infeasible, the skill flags this but cannot reset it.
- For SOC 2 and similar non-financial audits, the request types are familiar (evidence, walkthrough, sample) but the evidence content (security controls, incident response, vendor management) is outside the financial-statement focus. The skill structures the response; the security/IT team supplies the evidence content.
- For tax audits and regulatory audits, the formal structure of the engagement may include privilege considerations (especially for legal representations) that the skill does not address. Coordinate with legal counsel.
- The skill assumes the auditor is acting in good faith. Adversarial audits (regulator investigation, dispute-driven audit) may require different posture, especially around response timing and assertion scoping.
- The skill does not draft management representation letter content beyond standard structure. Specific assertions in the letter need controller and legal review.
- Sampling responses assume the auditor has selected the sample. The skill does not advise on whether the auditor's sampling methodology is appropriate; that is the auditor's professional judgment.
- For multi-entity, multi-jurisdiction audits with complex consolidation, the skill organizes the entity-level response; the consolidation-level response (eliminations, currency translation) requires consolidation-domain expertise.
- The skill does not store or transmit any actual evidence. Companies should use the auditor's secure portal or an encrypted file-share for actual transmission.
