# Data Classification and Retention Policy Author

## When to use

Use this skill when an organization needs a **defensible, automatable** classification + retention policy — not a 40-page Word doc that nobody applies. The output is a four-tier sensitivity scheme, a retention schedule keyed off the tier, an automated deletion design that survives audit, and a legal-hold escape hatch that doesn't accidentally exempt the entire warehouse.

Trigger phrases include:

- "data classification policy"
- "sensitivity tiers"
- "PII inventory / PII tagging"
- "retention schedule"
- "GDPR / CCPA deletion"
- "right to be forgotten"
- "legal hold"
- "purge / deletion job"
- "data minimization"

Do **not** use to design tests for compliance attestation (that's audit-control design) or to author contracts (route to `data-contract-author`). This skill produces the policy that contracts and catalogs *reference*.

## How to apply

Author the policy in **six parts**: tiers, mapping, retention, deletion, legal hold, and audit. The tiers and the deletion design are the parts most often botched.

### 1. The four-tier classification scheme

Use four tiers. Two tiers is too coarse (everything ends up "internal"); five or more is unmaintainable.

| Tier | Examples | Access default | Encryption | Where allowed |
| ---- | -------- | -------------- | ---------- | ------------- |
| **Public** | Marketing site copy, published research, public product catalog | World-readable | TLS in transit; rest optional | Anywhere |
| **Internal** | Org chart, internal docs, anonymized aggregate metrics, non-customer telemetry | All employees by default | Encrypted at rest | Approved systems only |
| **Confidential** | Customer PII (email, name, phone, address), employee records, business secrets, contracts | Need-to-know, named teams | Encrypted at rest; key access logged | Approved systems, no copy to laptops |
| **Restricted** | Payment card data, full government IDs, health records, biometrics, secrets/credentials, authentication material | Named individuals + manager approval; ticketed access | Encrypted at rest **and** application-layer (field-level) | A short, named whitelist of systems |

Rules of construction:

- **Tier up on ambiguity.** When a field could be Confidential or Restricted, classify Restricted. The cost of over-classifying a column is small; the cost of under-classifying a Social Security column is career-defining.
- **Derived data inherits the maximum tier of its inputs.** A view joining Internal and Confidential is Confidential. Aggregations that prevent re-identification can tier down — but require a documented k-anonymity / suppression rule, not a vibe.
- **Free text is Confidential by default.** Customer support transcripts, internal Slack exports, and any column called `notes` likely contain PII; treat them as Confidential until proven otherwise.
- **Secrets and credentials are always Restricted.** API keys, tokens, passwords, signing material — never co-mingled with other data; never readable by analysts; ideally not in the warehouse at all.

### 2. Mapping data to tiers

Three complementary techniques; use all three.

- **Schema-time declaration**: every contract and catalog entry has a `classification` field. New columns without one fail CI. This is the only sustainable mechanism — automated discovery alone never reaches steady state.
- **Discovery (name + sample heuristics)**: regex on column names (`email`, `ssn`, `dob`, `card_pan`), pattern matchers on a sample of values (Luhn for card numbers, e164 for phones), name-entity recognizers on free text. Use to **bootstrap** and **find drift**; never as the sole classification.
- **Lineage propagation**: any downstream dataset auto-inherits the max-tier of its inputs, surfaced in the catalog. Producers can tier down with a documented justification (anonymization step, aggregation rule). Reviewed by the privacy steward.

A column's final tier is `MAX(declared, discovered, inherited)`. Resolve disputes via the steward review cadence.

### 3. Retention schedule, keyed off classification

Retention rules must (a) be **per-tier defaults**, (b) override-able per-dataset for explicit business or regulatory reasons, and (c) **deletable** without ambiguity.

Default schedule:

| Tier | Default retention | Override rules |
| ---- | ----------------- | -------------- |
| Public | Indefinite | None needed |
| Internal | 2 years rolling | Up to 7 years with manager approval |
| Confidential | **Purpose-bounded**: retain only as long as the declared business purpose; default 13 months for customer PII, 7 years for accounting records | Override requires Privacy + Legal sign-off; document the lawful basis |
| Restricted | **Minimum necessary**: payment data per PCI rules; health data per HIPAA; secrets rotated < 90 days | No override that extends; only overrides that shorten |

Anchor every retention rule to **what triggers the countdown**:

- **From creation** (default): `now() - created_at >= retention`.
- **From last activity**: useful for accounts ("delete 24 months after last login").
- **From event** (e.g., account closure, contract termination): the cleanest anchor for customer data; requires emitting the event reliably.
- **From regulatory clock**: "7 years from end of fiscal year"; requires a separate clock.

Common regulatory anchors to encode (verify your jurisdiction):

- **SOX**: financial records 7 years.
- **GDPR / CCPA**: customer PII tied to purpose; right-to-be-forgotten on request.
- **HIPAA**: PHI 6 years from creation or last effective date (US).
- **PCI-DSS**: cardholder data — keep the minimum needed; PAN never stored unencrypted; consider not storing at all (tokenize).

### 4. Automated deletion

A retention policy that depends on humans running a quarterly script is not a retention policy. Design for automation.

- **Soft-delete first**: mark rows `deleted_at`; readers filter; auditors can confirm what was due for deletion.
- **Hard-delete on schedule**: a scheduled job purges rows whose `deleted_at + grace_period < now()`. Grace period is typically 30 days for confidential, 7 days for restricted, longer where regulators require recoverability.
- **Cascading through lineage**: when a record is deleted in the source, derived datasets must delete or anonymize the affected rows. Without lineage and an `id` mapping table, GDPR right-to-be-forgotten becomes a manual scavenger hunt.
- **Anonymization as an alternative to deletion** where allowed: replace PII with a stable surrogate token, blank free-text columns, salt-hash any joinable identifiers.
- **Immutable logs** (event streams, audit logs): deletion must address the log's retention separately — usually by **rotating** logs out of immutable storage after their statutory window, not by editing in place.
- **Backups**: retention applies to backups too. Document the backup lifecycle (e.g., daily backups retained 35 days), reconcile with deletion SLA (GDPR is typically 30 days — many shops fail audit on backups). Either shorten backup retention or use **deterministic restore-and-delete** (restore, re-run deletion, re-snapshot).

Acceptance tests for the deletion design:

- A test row inserted with classification=Confidential and `created_at = now() - 14_months` is gone (or anonymized) from prod **and** from any derived table within 7 days.
- A right-to-be-forgotten request for a known test user is honored end-to-end (warehouse, lake, BI cache, backups) within the SLA, evidenced by query logs.
- Deletion job failures **alert** within the same SLA window; missing a quarter of deletions silently is the worst failure mode.

### 5. Legal hold

Legal hold is the override that pauses deletion when litigation or regulatory action requires evidence preservation. It is also the loophole that swallows policies — design it carefully.

- **Hold = a tag on records (or a query predicate) that exempts them from deletion.** Never a global "pause the deletion job" — that exempts everything and gets forgotten.
- **Scoped**: holds name (a) the matter, (b) the affected dataset(s), (c) the row predicate (e.g., `customer_id IN (...)` or a date range), and (d) the responsible counsel.
- **Time-bounded by default**: a hold has an expiry. Extensions require a documented renewal. "Indefinite" holds are a code smell.
- **Audited**: every hold and its release is a logged event. Counsel signs the apply and the release.
- **Resumes the clock, not restarts it**: when a hold lifts, deletion proceeds from the previously-scheduled date, not from "now + retention". Otherwise holds become indefinite extensions.
- **Read-only side path** for hold-tagged data after its normal retention: keep it accessible only to counsel, with access logged.

### 6. Audit and review

The policy that isn't audited isn't enforced.

- **Coverage SLOs**: ≥ 99% of datasets carry a classification tag; ≥ 95% have a retention rule; ≥ 99% of Confidential / Restricted have an owner and a retention rule.
- **Quarterly access review**: who has access to Restricted; remove dormant grants.
- **Annual review**: re-walk the highest-tier datasets; confirm business purpose; re-confirm retention; re-confirm classification (data does change tier — anonymization can drop it, new joins can lift it).
- **Deletion evidence**: a queryable log of "what was deleted, when, by what rule". Auditors will ask.
- **Discovery scan**: schedule a name-and-sample scan that flags suspected PII in datasets classified as Internal. Treat findings as incidents.

### Worked policy (excerpt)

```yaml
tiers: [public, internal, confidential, restricted]

defaults_by_tier:
  public:      { retention: indefinite,  access: world, encryption_at_rest: optional }
  internal:    { retention: 2y,          access: all_employees, encryption_at_rest: required }
  confidential: { retention: 13mo_or_purpose, access: named_team, encryption_at_rest: required, key_access_logged: true }
  restricted:  { retention: minimum_necessary, access: named_individual_w_approval, encryption_at_rest: required, field_level_encryption: true }

classification_methods:
  - schema_declaration  # required in every contract; new column without it fails CI
  - discovery_scan      # weekly; columns matching email/ssn/card patterns flagged
  - lineage_propagation # max-tier inheritance; downgrade requires sign-off

retention_overrides:
  - dataset: orders.line_items_v1
    classification: confidential
    retention: 7y_fiscal      # SOX
    anchor: end_of_fiscal_year
    approved_by: privacy_steward + legal
  - dataset: support.tickets
    classification: confidential
    retention: 24mo
    anchor: ticket_closed_at

deletion:
  strategy: soft_then_hard
  soft_delete_filter: "deleted_at IS NULL"
  grace_period:
    confidential: 30d
    restricted: 7d
  cascade: via_lineage_with_id_map
  backups:
    retention: 35d
    rtbf_method: restore_then_redelete
  anonymization_allowed: yes_with_documented_method

legal_hold:
  carrier: row_tag(legal_hold_matter_id)
  scope_required: [matter_id, dataset, predicate, counsel]
  default_expiry: 1y
  renewal: counsel_sign
  on_release: resume_clock_from_original_due_date
  log: append_only_immutable

audit:
  coverage_slos:
    classification_tagged: 0.99
    retention_set: 0.95
    confidential_with_owner: 0.99
  reviews:
    quarterly: [restricted_access_review, hold_review]
    annual:    [tier1_dataset_walk, classification_drift_scan]
  deletion_evidence_log: enabled
```

## Inputs

- **scope** (required): systems, datasets, jurisdictions, regulated frameworks.
- **existing** (optional): current tags, policies, prior incidents.
- **data_types** (optional): categories present (customer PII, payments, health, employee).
- **constraints** (optional): tooling, residency, immutability, audit cadence.

## Outputs

- The four-tier scheme tailored to the org (with examples mapped to the actual datasets).
- Classification mechanisms — schema declaration, discovery, lineage propagation.
- Retention schedule per tier, with documented overrides per regulated dataset.
- Automated-deletion design: soft + hard, cascade, backups, anonymization.
- Legal-hold workflow: scope, expiry, audit trail, release rules.
- Audit cadence + coverage SLOs + deletion-evidence requirements.
- A 90-day rollout: instrument classification, run discovery, wire deletion job, dry-run, enable.

## Examples

> "B2C app, EU + US customers, Snowflake + S3, GDPR + CCPA, no policy today."

Four tiers as above. Confidential default for customer PII; 13-month retention from last login. Restricted for any payment data (recommend tokenization — don't store PAN). Discovery scan on all Internal-tagged columns weekly. Deletion job: soft-delete at 13mo, hard-purge at 13mo + 30d. Right-to-be-forgotten endpoint writes to a deletion queue; cascade via lineage to derived tables; backups handled via shortened 35-day window. Legal hold tagged at row level, scoped to matter id, default 12-month expiry.

> "B2B SaaS, SOC 2 in flight, financial records 7y, customer support transcripts."

Confidential for support transcripts (free text → assume PII). 7y for accounting per SOX, anchored to end-of-fiscal-year. Quarterly access review on Restricted (production database creds). Hold workflow tied to legal SaaS; require counsel sign-off on apply and release.

> "Healthcare-adjacent product, US, no PHI but we collect demographic + health-self-report."

Treat the demographic + self-report fields as Restricted to be conservative; document the lawful basis. 6y retention. Field-level encryption on the self-report column. Backup window aligned with retention SLA (don't keep 1y of backups if retention is 6y from event; align or document the gap).

## Limitations

- This skill produces policy, not legal advice. The retention numbers above are common defaults; **engage counsel** for any regulated dataset before enabling automated deletion.
- HIPAA, PCI-DSS, FedRAMP, and sector-specific regimes have requirements beyond what's encoded here. Treat the output as a starting point, not a compliance artifact.
- Deletion through immutable event streams and ML training corpora is a hard problem — flag any such systems and plan a separate spike.
- The policy is only as good as the catalog underneath it; if classification can't be discovered or stored against datasets, this becomes paperwork. Pair with `data-catalog-architect`.
